Health Care Finance: Whose Balance Sheet Are You On?

The phrase covers three unrelated subjects, and only one of them changes what you personally owe. That one is the point at which a hospital bill stops being a hospital bill and becomes a credit product held by somebody else.

It matters because the strongest protections in this area are duties of the hospital: a written financial assistance policy, a cap on what an eligible patient may be charged, and a 120-day pause before extraordinary collection actions, with an application window running to 240 days. Move the obligation and you are in a different body of law.

Three subjects, one phrase

Type it and you get a patient-lending company, a professional association for provider finance staff, a trade news service, an international organisation and a university programme. Those are not variations on one topic. They are the consumer question, the practitioner discipline and the policy field, sharing a name.

The practitioner discipline is real work — revenue cycle, contracting, cost accounting — and the policy field has its own literature. Neither answers the question most people arrive with, which is what to do about a bill. So this guide follows the consumer thread, and the organising question is ownership: whose balance sheet is the obligation sitting on, and what attaches to it there.

What a charitable hospital already owes you

26 U.S.C. 501(r)(1) states that a hospital organisation shall not be treated as described in subsection (c)(3) unless the organization meets four sets of requirements: the community health needs assessment, the financial assistance policy, the requirements on charges, and the billing and collection requirement. Tax-exempt status is conditioned on them.

The policy requirement is fleshed out at 26 CFR 1.501(r)-4. The financial assistance policy must apply to all emergency and other medically necessary care provided by the hospital facility, and must state the eligibility criteria for financial assistance and whether such assistance includes free or discounted care, the basis for calculating amounts charged to patients, the method for applying for financial assistance, and the actions that may be taken in the event of nonpayment. It must also list providers other than the hospital facility delivering care covered by it, and it must be widely publicised.

A separate emergency policy is required at (c): a written policy requiring the facility to provide, without discrimination, care for emergency medical conditions regardless of whether they are FAP-eligible, and prohibiting actions that discourage people from seeking emergency care, such as by demanding that emergency department patients pay before receiving treatment.

The charge cap most people never claim

26 CFR 1.501(r)-5 limits what an eligible patient may be charged. For emergency or other medically necessary care, a facility must charge a FAP-eligible individual not more than the amounts generally billed to individuals who have insurance covering such care. For any other medical care it provides them, it must charge less than the gross charges.

The significance is in the comparator. The cap is not a discount off the chargemaster at the hospital’s discretion; it is pegged to what insured patients are billed, calculated by a prescribed method. The five different prices a hospital publishes, and which one applies to whom, are set out in our guide to the prices already published free.

Note also what triggers it: being FAP-eligible, which is a determination, not a request. Someone who never applies is never eligible, and the cap never engages. That is the single most common way this protection goes unused.

The 120 days and the 240 days

26 CFR 1.501(r)-6 governs billing and collection. A facility must make reasonable efforts to determine whether an individual is FAP-eligible before initiating extraordinary collection actions — a defined category that includes reporting adverse information to credit agencies, deferring or denying medically necessary care because of nonpayment of a prior bill, legal actions such as liens and garnishment, and selling the debt.

One route to reasonable efforts requires that the facility refrains from initiating such ECAs… for at least 120 days from the date the hospital facility provides the first post-discharge billing statement. And the application deadline in the notification route must be no earlier than the later of 30 days after the date that the written notice is provided or 240 days after the date that the first post-discharge billing statement for the previously provided care was provided.

Two dates, then, both measured from the same event: the first post-discharge statement. Keep it. It is the anchor for every deadline in this section, and it is the document most likely to be discarded as routine.

When the debt is sold

Selling an individual’s debt is itself an extraordinary collection action, with a carve-out that is worth reading as a specification. A sale is outside the definition where the hospital has a binding written agreement with the purchaser that prohibits the purchaser from engaging in extraordinary collection actions, limits the interest that may be charged, makes the debt returnable or recallable if the individual is determined FAP-eligible, and ensures the individual does not pay more than they are personally responsible for.

That list is effectively a statement of what would otherwise be lost. Read backwards, it says: without such an agreement, a sale can put the obligation somewhere the interest is uncapped, the collection tools are unrestricted, and a later eligibility determination has nowhere to land.

Which is why ownership is the right frame for this whole subject. The question is not only how much is owed but to whom, because the protections are attached to the party, not to the amount.

The moment it becomes a loan

Third-party financing changes the character of the obligation. 12 CFR 1026.2(a)(12) defines consumer credit as credit offered or extended to a consumer primarily for personal, family, or household purposes, and (a)(17)(i) defines a creditor as a person who regularly extends consumer credit that is subject to a finance charge or is payable by written agreement in more than four installments (not including a down payment).

Those two features are the practical test to apply to anything offered at a registration desk. Is there a finance charge, and is it payable in more than four instalments by written agreement? A genuine interest-free arrangement in four or fewer payments is a different animal from a credit product, and the difference is visible in the paperwork rather than in the sales language.

Neither answer is automatically bad. The point is that once the obligation is a consumer credit obligation to a finance company, the hospital’s policy, its charge cap and its collection clocks are no longer the operative rules for it.

The order to do things in

Sequence carries most of the value here. Ask for the financial assistance policy first, before agreeing to any payment arrangement, because the policy has to state its own eligibility criteria and application method and because eligibility is what activates the charge cap.

Keep the first post-discharge billing statement, since both clocks run from it. Apply within the window rather than waiting to see whether the bill is pursued; the determination is what protects you, and a determination made late protects less.

Only then consider financing, and read it against the two-part credit test. Signing a credit agreement before an eligibility determination converts a protected hospital obligation into an unprotected consumer one, which is the reverse of the order most people are offered it in.

What this does not cover

It does not apply to every hospital. Section 501(r) governs charitable hospital organisations; a for-profit or a government facility sits outside it, and other rules and state law fill in differently. What binds each kind of facility is mapped in the federal list, and what binds each.

It also does not tell you what you will owe in the first place. That depends on your plan’s design, the dials described in the dial nobody caps, and the separate estimate a self-pay patient can demand, covered in the estimate you can demand. Which regulator supervises your coverage is sorted in which regulator is yours.

What the rules above do give you is an ownership map. Before agreeing to anything, work out which entity holds the obligation and which of the protections in the table travels with it. That question is answerable from documents you are entitled to have, and it is the part of health care finance that is actually about you.

Questions people actually ask

What does health care finance mean?

At least three things: the financing offered to a patient for a bill, the professional discipline of managing a provider's revenue and costs, and the study of how a country pays for health care overall. The search results mix all three. The one with the most concrete personal consequences is the first, because it changes who holds your obligation.

What must a charitable hospital have in place?

Under 26 U.S.C. 501(r)(1) a hospital organization is not treated as tax-exempt under 501(c)(3) unless it meets the community health needs assessment requirements, the financial assistance policy requirements, the requirements on charges, and the billing and collection requirement. The financial assistance policy itself must state eligibility criteria and whether assistance is free or discounted, the basis for calculating amounts charged, the method for applying, and the actions that may be taken in the event of nonpayment.

Is there a cap on what I can be charged?

For people eligible under the hospital's financial assistance policy, yes. Under 26 CFR 1.501(r)-5 a hospital facility must limit charges for emergency or other medically necessary care provided to FAP-eligible individuals to not more than the amounts generally billed to individuals who have insurance covering such care, and must charge less than gross charges for any other medical care it provides them.

How long before a hospital can start collection actions?

Under 26 CFR 1.501(r)-6 a hospital facility must make reasonable efforts to determine whether an individual is eligible for assistance before initiating extraordinary collection actions, which includes refraining from those actions for at least 120 days from the date the first post-discharge billing statement is provided. The application deadline in the notification route must be no earlier than the later of 30 days after the written notice or 240 days after that first statement.

Can a hospital sell my bill to someone else?

Selling an individual's debt to another party is itself an extraordinary collection action under 26 CFR 1.501(r)-6, unless the sale falls within the described exception. That exception requires a binding written agreement with the purchaser that prohibits the purchaser from engaging in extraordinary collection actions, limits interest, makes the debt returnable or recallable if the individual is determined FAP-eligible, and ensures the individual does not pay more than they are personally responsible for.

When does a payment plan become a loan?

When someone is acting as a creditor extending consumer credit. Under 12 CFR 1026.2(a)(17)(i) a creditor is a person who regularly extends consumer credit that is subject to a finance charge or is payable by written agreement in more than four installments, and (a)(12) defines consumer credit as credit offered or extended to a consumer primarily for personal, family, or household purposes. Those two features, a finance charge or more than four installments, are the practical test.

Why does it matter who holds the debt?

Because the hospital-specific protections attach to the hospital obligation. The financial assistance policy, the charge cap for FAP-eligible individuals and the collection timing rules are duties of the hospital facility under section 501(r). A third-party financing product is a different obligation to a different party, governed by consumer credit law instead, so the sequence in which you apply for assistance and accept financing is consequential.
