Health Information Management Programs: The Code Underneath the Name

Last verified
31 Aug 2026
Jurisdiction
United States, federal
Data sources
NCES Classification of Instructional Programs; 20 U.S.C. 1094
Sources
6 cited
Reading time
10 min

Every programme you are comparing already carries a six-digit federal code, and the code has a published definition the institution does not write. Health information programmes sit in family 51.07, Health and Medical Administrative Services, which contains 24 codes.

Six of those describe handling health information, and they are not the same job. Two carry the name directly: 51.0706 for the administrator and 51.0707 for the technician. Read their definitions side by side and the reporting line between the two roles is already written into federal text.

Comparing programmes without a common unit

Programme names are marketing. One institution says health information management, another says health informatics and information management, a third says medical records technology, and a fourth folds the whole thing into a health administration track. Nothing in those names is standardised.

Underneath, there is something that is. Every programme is filed under a code from the Classification of Instructional Programs, and each code carries a definition written centrally rather than by the institution. That makes the code the only common unit available for comparison — the thing that survives translation between one prospectus and the next.

Every programme already carries a code

The family that matters here is 51.07, Health and Medical Administrative Services, whose own entry simply states that instructional content for the group is defined in codes 51.0701 - 51.0799. The detail lives in the six-digit entries beneath it.

There are twenty-four of them, running from 51.0701 to 51.0723 plus the residual 51.0799. They cover ground as varied as ward clerking, medical reception, long term care administration, regulatory affairs and clinical research coordination. A prospective student searching one phrase is looking at a slice of that list without being told where the slice ends.

The size of the family is itself information. Twenty-four recognised programme types sit between clinical care and the front office, which is a record of how finely that work has been divided over time. It also means the phrase in your search box is not a category the classification recognises. It is a description, and it lands somewhere inside a two-dozen-item list without telling you where.

The two that carry the name

51.0706, Health Information/Medical Records Administration/Administrator, is defined as a program that prepares individuals to plan, design, and manage systems, processes, and facilities used to collect, store, secure, retrieve, analyze, and transmit medical records and other health information used by clinical professionals and health care organizations, with instruction spanning biomedical and clinical sciences, information technology, database management, clinical research methodologies, office management, legal requirements and professional standards.

51.0707, Health Information/Medical Records Technology/Technician, is defined as a program that prepares individuals, under the supervision of health information administrators and other professionals, to construct medical records and clinical databases, perform manipulations on retrieved data, control the security and quality of records, and supervise data entry and technical maintenance personnel.

The verbs separate them cleanly. 51.0706 is plan, design and manage — systems and facilities. 51.0707 is construct, manipulate, control and supervise — records and the people entering them. Both are real jobs; they are simply different ones.

The hierarchy written into the definition

One clause in 51.0707 is worth pausing on: under the supervision of health information administrators. That is the role 51.0706 prepares people to hold.

So the federal taxonomy does not merely list two adjacent programmes. It states the reporting line between them, in the definition of the junior code, before any institution has written a syllabus. Very few pairs of CIP codes do this, and it tells you something the prospectus will not: these are not two routes to the same destination but two rungs, and the choice of rung is being made at enrolment. What the senior rung is accountable for is set out in our guide to the duties attached to the chair.

Six codes, one search phrase

Four more codes in the family describe information work, and each is a distinct occupation.

51.0713, Medical Insurance Coding Specialist/Coder prepares individuals to perform specialized data entry, classification, and record-keeping procedures related to medical diagnostic, treatment, billing, and insurance documentation, with instruction in data classification and coding and in regulations relating to Medicare and insurance documentation. Coding is a component of health information work, but a programme filed here is a coding programme, not a management one.

51.0723, Healthcare Information Privacy Assurance and Security prepares individuals for careers in designing, implementing, and administering comprehensive privacy and security protection programs in all types of healthcare organizations. Privacy and security have their own code, which is the clearest evidence available that the field has specialised past a single degree.

51.0721, Disease Registry Data Management and 51.0708, Medical Transcription/Transcriptionist complete the set. Registry work and transcription both handle the clinical record, and neither is what most people mean when they type the search phrase.

The six also span the entire range of time and money in the field. A transcription or coding certificate can be measured in months; the administrator code is normally a baccalaureate programme. One search phrase therefore covers options that differ by a factor of several in both duration and total cost, and the code is the quickest way to establish which end of that range a given programme sits at.

Why the code is on file at all

Coded reporting is a condition of participating in federal student aid. Under 20 U.S.C. 1094(a)(17) an institution agrees that the institution will complete surveys conducted as a part of the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System (IPEDS) or any other Federal postsecondary institution data collection effort, as designated by the Secretary, in a timely manner and to the satisfaction of the Secretary.

That single clause is why the comparison is possible. The institution is not describing itself voluntarily in a vocabulary it chose; it is filing under a taxonomy it does not control, as a term of its participation. Marketing copy has no such discipline behind it.

Using the code when you compare

Ask the admissions office which CIP code the programme is reported under, and at which award level. It is an administrative fact the institution already holds, and a programme that cannot answer quickly has told you something about its own record-keeping.

Then read the definition of that code, not the brochure, and check that the verbs match the work you want. If you want to run a department, the definition should say plan, design and manage. If the code that comes back is 51.0713 or 51.0708, the programme is a good programme for a different job.

Watch for institutions running more than one programme in the family. A department may offer a certificate under one code and a degree under another while marketing both under a single departmental name, which is entirely legitimate and entirely confusing from outside. The enquiry that resolves it is the same one asked twice: which code, and which level, for this specific award.

Finally, hold the code steady while everything else varies. Two programmes under the same code with different names are more alike than two programmes sharing a name under different codes — which is the exact inversion of how the search results are ordered.

What the code does not tell you

It says nothing about quality, cost, completion or where graduates end up. It is a subject classification, and a poor programme and an excellent one file under the same six digits.

It also does not settle examination eligibility, which runs through programmatic accreditation rather than federal classification and is the decisive check for anyone aiming at a credential. That system, and the exclusion that catches people who assume two similar programme names lead to the same place, is covered separately in the name binds.

Use the two together and the sequence is simple. The CIP code tells you what the programme is. The accreditation status tells you what it qualifies you to sit. The wider map of who does what in the field, and what each part pays, is in roles, pay and credentials and in which code you are in.

Questions people actually ask

What is a CIP code and why does it matter for choosing a programme?

The Classification of Instructional Programs is the federal taxonomy every institution uses to file its programmes with the Department of Education. Each programme is reported under a six-digit code with a published definition, so the code is a common unit that lets programmes with different marketing names be compared. Health information programmes sit in the 51.07 family, Health and Medical Administrative Services.

What is CIP 51.0706?

Health Information/Medical Records Administration/Administrator. Its published definition is a program that prepares individuals to plan, design, and manage systems, processes, and facilities used to collect, store, secure, retrieve, analyze, and transmit medical records and other health information used by clinical professionals and health care organizations. The verbs are plan, design and manage.

What is CIP 51.0707?

Health Information/Medical Records Technology/Technician. Its definition is a program that prepares individuals, under the supervision of health information administrators and other professionals, to construct medical records and clinical databases, perform manipulations on retrieved data, control the security and quality of records, and supervise data entry and technical maintenance personnel.

What is the difference between the two codes?

The supervision clause. 51.0707 explicitly prepares people to work under the supervision of health information administrators, which is the role 51.0706 prepares people to hold. The federal taxonomy therefore encodes the reporting line between the two, before any institution writes a course catalogue.

Are there other codes that could be called a health information programme?

Yes. Within the 51.07 family, alongside 51.0706 and 51.0707, there are Medical Transcription/Transcriptionist at 51.0708, Medical Insurance Coding Specialist/Coder at 51.0713, Disease Registry Data Management at 51.0721, and Healthcare Information Privacy Assurance and Security at 51.0723. Six codes describe information-handling work, out of twenty-four in the family.

Why do institutions report a code at all?

It is a condition of federal student aid participation. Under 20 U.S.C. 1094(a)(17) an institution agrees that it will complete surveys conducted as a part of the Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System or any other Federal postsecondary institution data collection effort, as designated by the Secretary, in a timely manner and to the satisfaction of the Secretary. The coded reporting is not optional for participating institutions.

Does the code tell me whether a programme is any good?

No. A CIP code describes subject matter, not quality, accreditation status or outcomes. It tells you what the institution says the programme is, in a vocabulary that is the same everywhere. Accreditation and examination eligibility are separate systems maintained by different bodies and must be checked independently.
