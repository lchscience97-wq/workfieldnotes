Health Center 3 is at 555 S. 43rd St., Philadelphia, PA 19104, with published numbers (215) 685-7504 and (215) 685-7506. It is one of the health centers operated directly by the Philadelphia Department of Public Health.

The information most people want next is scattered across four separate City pages: the roster, the fee policy, the service list, and the fact that these municipal centers are also federally qualified health centers. This page puts all four together, and explains why the numbering skips.

Health Center 3, in one place

The City of Philadelphia publishes Health Center 3 at 555 S. 43rd St., Philadelphia, PA 19104, in West Philadelphia, with two telephone numbers listed: (215) 685-7504 and (215) 685-7506.

It appears on the City's roster among the centers described as providing a full range of primary medical and support services to registered patients, as distinct from the one site on the roster limited to a single service. The City's own instruction before visiting is unambiguous and worth following: call ahead to make sure the service you need is available when you plan to visit. Service availability at a given center on a given day is not published centrally.

Why the numbers skip

The published roster is not a contiguous sequence. It runs Health Center 1 through Health Center 6, then jumps to Health Center 10, and includes two centers listed by name rather than number: the Mattie L. Humphrey Health Center on E. Chelten Ave. and the Strawberry Mansion Health Center on West Dauphin St.

This matters practically rather than historically. The numbers are legacy district identifiers, not an index, so a search for a number in the gap returns directory pages, third-party listings and stale references rather than a City page — because there is no City page to return. If you have been given a number that does not appear on the roster, the reliable move is to search by street address or to call the center you can identify nearest to it.

It also means the count of buildings and the count of full-service centers differ. The City lists nine sites but states that eight City health centers provide a full range of primary medical and support services to registered patients. The difference is Health Center 1, at 1930 S Broad St., which the City lists for urgent care STD testing and treatment only.

City-run and federally qualified at the same time

This is the fact the City publishes least prominently and that changes the most.

Philadelphia's own Find a Clinic handout listing federally qualified health centers in the city includes the AHS Health Centers numbered 1 through 10 and the Strawberry Mansion Health Center alongside independent non-profit organisations such as GPHA, PHMC, Project HOME, Spectrum Health Services, Esperanza Health Center, Delaware Valley Community Health, the Family Practice and Counseling Network and Philadelphia FIGHT.

A municipal health department inside the federal Health Center Program is not an anomaly. HRSA recognises three categories of eligible organisation for the programme: private non-profit entities, public agencies including state, county, city and municipal governments and their health departments, and tribal or urban Indian organisations. Philadelphia sits in the second category. Our guide to what the federal test actually is works through the statute in full.

What the dual status is worth to a patient

Section 330 of the Public Health Service Act, codified at 42 U.S.C. §254b, attaches conditions to the designation, and three of them are things a patient can rely on rather than hope for.

The centre must serve all residents of the area served by the center, which is why the City's handout describes federally qualified health centers as serving people of all ages regardless of their ability to pay or health insurance status. It must maintain a schedule of fees together with a corresponding schedule of discounts adjusted on the basis of ability to pay. And the required primary health services under 254b(b)(1)(A) — family medicine, internal medicine, pediatrics, obstetrics and gynecology, diagnostic laboratory and radiologic services, preventive services including prenatal care, cancer screening, immunizations and dental screenings, emergency medical services and pharmaceutical services — must be available within the organisation's approved scope.

The scale nationally puts the local roster in context. In 2025, Health Center Program award recipients reported 32,746,392 patients and look-alikes a further 1,650,551, for 34,396,943 in total, with 89.97 percent of award recipients' patients at or below 200 percent of the federal poverty level.

What is actually on offer

The City's published service list for its health centers is unusually long for a municipal programme, and it is worth reading before assuming a service requires a hospital referral.

It covers diagnosis and treatment of chronic and acute illness, medical checkups, medications, breast exams, cervical and colon and lung and prostate cancer screenings, mammography, developmental assessments, cholesterol and diabetes and hypertension screenings, hepatitis C screening, obesity screening and dietician consultations, nutrition counselling, family planning and gynaecological care, prenatal care and breastfeeding support, pediatric care, dental care, behavioural health services, medication-assisted treatment for opioid use disorder, HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis, STI testing and treatment, immunisations and vaccines, laboratory tests, x-rays, tuberculosis testing, tobacco cessation help and social work support.

Dental care and behavioural health being on that list is the part most people do not expect from a city clinic. Neither is universally available at every site, which is what the call-ahead instruction is there to resolve.

Cost, insurance and the sliding fee

The City's fee statement is one sentence: if you are uninsured, the centers will charge a small fee based on family size and income. That is a sliding fee discount, and it is a federal condition of the designation rather than a discretionary local policy.

On coverage, the City states that health centers accept Medicare, Medicaid, HMO plans and most other insurance options. Being insured is not a disqualification and does not push you toward a different door. Nationally most health center patients are insured: in 2025 award recipients reported 47.92 percent of patients on Medicaid or CHIP against 17.21 percent uninsured.

What the City does not publish on that page is the discount schedule itself — the table keyed to household income and family size. Asking for it by name at the front desk is the reliable way to find out what a visit will cost before it happens, and a centre operating under the designation is required to have one. Our guide to six routes and their clocks compares this against the alternatives.

What "registered patient" means

The City's phrasing is that the full range of services goes to registered patients, and its departmental page repeats that City-operated health centers provide primary medical care and support services to registered patients.

Neither page publishes a registration procedure. The instruction given instead is to call ahead. In practice the phrase describes being enrolled with a centre for ongoing primary care rather than attending once, and some public health services listed by the department — vaccination among them — are described without that condition attached. If you need one visit rather than a medical home, saying so on the phone is the fastest route to the right answer.

The centers that are not city-run

Philadelphia's federally qualified health centers extend well beyond the City's own roster, and the distinction is worth holding onto when comparing options. GPHA, PHMC, Project HOME, Y-HEP, Spectrum Health Services, the Family Practice and Counseling Network, Delaware Valley Community Health, QCHC, Esperanza Health Center, Covenant House Health Services and Philadelphia FIGHT all appear on the City's own FQHC handout.

They carry the same federal obligations as the City centers — the discount schedule, the serve-everyone requirement, the required service list — but they are separate organisations with their own governance, sites and hours. A numbered center is a City center; a named organisation on that list is not, and the two are frequently confused in third-party directories.

Before you go

Call the published number for the specific site and confirm three things: that the service you need is offered there, whether you need to be a registered patient for it, and what you will be charged given your insurance status and household size. Bring proof of income and family size if you are uninsured, because that is what the discount schedule is applied against.

If the number you were given does not match anything on the roster above, it is almost certainly an outdated district number rather than a closed site. Search by street or call the nearest listed center, which will route you. And if you are not sure a facility is in this programme at all, the question to ask is not whether it is a clinic but whether it is federally qualified — a distinction our guide to the four legal forms of the word clinic sets out.

Questions people actually ask

Where is Health Center 3 in Philadelphia?

The City of Philadelphia lists Health Center 3 at 555 S. 43rd St., Philadelphia, PA 19104, with published telephone numbers (215) 685-7504 and (215) 685-7506. It is one of the City health centers operated by the Philadelphia Department of Public Health, and the City advises calling ahead to make sure the service you need is available when you plan to visit.

Why do the health center numbers skip?

The City's published roster runs Health Center 1 through Health Center 6, then Health Center 10, alongside two centers listed by name rather than number: the Mattie L. Humphrey Health Center and the Strawberry Mansion Health Center. The numbers are a legacy district identifier rather than a contiguous index, which is why searching by a number in the gap returns nothing useful. Searching for a center by name or address is more reliable.

Are the City health centers federally qualified health centers?

Yes. The City's own Find a Clinic handout listing federally qualified health centers in Philadelphia includes the AHS Health Centers numbered 1 through 10 and the Strawberry Mansion Health Center. That places a municipal health department inside the federal Health Center Program, which HRSA permits: public agencies, including city and county governments and their health departments, are one of the three categories of eligible organization.

How much does a visit cost if I have no insurance?

The City states that if you are uninsured, the centers will charge a small fee based on family size and income. That is a sliding fee discount, and it reflects a federal condition rather than a local courtesy: section 330 of the Public Health Service Act requires a schedule of fees together with a corresponding schedule of discounts adjusted on the basis of the patient's ability to pay.

Do the City health centers take insurance?

Yes. The City states that health centers accept Medicare, Medicaid, HMO plans, and most other insurance options. Having insurance does not disqualify you, and most health center patients nationally are insured. In 2025 Health Center Program awardees reported 47.92 percent of patients covered by Medicaid or CHIP and 17.21 percent with no insurance at all.

Which City health center is not full service?

Health Center 1, at 1930 S Broad St., is listed by the City for urgent care STD testing and treatment only. The City's wording is that eight City health centers provide a full range of primary medical and support services to registered patients, which is why the count of buildings on the roster and the count of full-service centers do not match.

What does registered patient mean here?

The City uses the phrase to describe people enrolled with a center for ongoing primary care, and states that the full range of primary medical and support services is provided to registered patients. It does not publish a registration procedure on the health centers page; it instructs you to call ahead to confirm the service you need is available. Certain public health services, such as vaccination, are described without that condition.
