Health Care Jobs: Two Tiers, $45,910 Apart

Last verified
7 Aug 2026
Jurisdiction
United States
Wage data
BLS, May 2024; projections 2024–34
Sources
6 cited
Reading time
9 min

"Health care jobs" is not one labour market. It is two, and the federal statistics report them separately. Healthcare practitioners and technical occupations had a May 2024 median annual wage of $83,090. Healthcare support occupations had $37,180.

The gap is $45,910 — nearly as large as the $49,500 median for all occupations, which sits between the two tiers. About 1.9 million openings a year are projected across healthcare, but which side of that line an opening falls on decides almost everything about it.

What a job board cannot show you

The results for this phrase are aggregators, recruitment services and individual health systems advertising their own vacancies. Only one federal source appears at all. Each of those is answering "what is open right now", which is a useful question and a different one from "what is this sector actually made of".

The distinction matters here more than in most fields, because healthcare contains two populations with very little in common except the buildings they work in. A posting will not tell you which one it belongs to. The federal classification will.

There is a second reason to start with the classification rather than the listings. Search volume for this phrase is dominated by people at the start of a decision, not the end of one — deciding whether to enter the sector at all, or which part of it to train for. A list of vacancies answers neither question, because vacancies are a snapshot of one week's demand filtered through whichever employers pay to advertise. The occupational data describes the standing structure that produces those vacancies, and it is the structure that determines what a training decision is worth five years later.

The line BLS draws through the sector

The Occupational Outlook Handbook reports healthcare in two groups, and publishes a separate median for each. Healthcare practitioners and technical occupations — the licensed and credentialed clinical roles — recorded a median annual wage of $83,090 in May 2024. Healthcare support occupations recorded $37,180. The median for all occupations was $49,500.

Three comparisons follow, and they are the frame for everything else on this page.

The practitioner tier sits $33,590 above the national median, about 67.9 per cent higher.

The support tier sits $12,320 below it, about 24.9 per cent lower.

The two tiers are $45,910 apart, a ratio of roughly 2.2 to 1. That single boundary explains more variation in healthcare pay than seniority, geography or employer type do.

The column that behaves least like the others is the last one. Turnover does not track pay, and it does not track growth: the highest-turnover row here is the lowest-paid, and the lowest-turnover row is the largest occupation in the table by a factor of four.

Where 1.9 million openings a year come from

BLS projects about 1.9 million openings each year on average across healthcare occupations from 2024 to 2034, arising from employment growth and the need to replace workers who leave the occupations permanently, with overall employment projected to grow much faster than the average for all occupations.

The two components behave differently. Growth openings appear where a service line is expanding; replacement openings appear where people leave. A high-growth occupation with low turnover advertises less often than its growth rate suggests, and a flat occupation with high turnover advertises constantly.

Registered nursing is the clearest case. It is projected to grow only 5 per cent, yet generates about 189,100 openings a year — more than any other row here — purely because 3,391,000 people hold the job and some fraction of them leave it every year. Growth rate and job availability are not the same measurement.

Growth is concentrated, not spread

The starkest pair in the table is registered nurses at 5 per cent against the advanced practice group at 35 per cent — a seven-fold difference between two occupations that share a professional lineage. BLS puts about 134,000 new jobs behind that 35 per cent.

The wage difference across the same step is $38,450 at the median — $93,600 to $132,050 — and within the advanced practice group the tenth percentile earned less than $98,520 while the ninetieth earned more than $217,270.

What separates the two is a master's degree plus licensure. That is the single highest- leverage transition visible in this data, and it is also the one that costs the most time before it pays anything.

The licence is the border

Read the entry column against the pay column and the boundary resolves into a licensing question rather than an education one.

Registered nurses must be licensed, which requires graduating from an approved programme and passing a qualifying examination. Advanced practice nurses need registered nurse licensure, an accredited graduate programme, a national certification examination and a state APRN licence, and requirements vary by state. For medical assistants, by contrast, most states do not require certification, though employers may prefer or require it.

That is the mechanism. A licence restricts who may perform the work; restricted supply supports the wage. Where no licence exists, the credential is a hiring signal competing against every other hiring signal. The same logic governs the management side of the sector, where only one job family is licensed at all — set out in where the administration jobs are.

What crossing the line actually takes

Three of the five rows above are reachable in two years or less of formal education: medical assistant on a postsecondary nondegree award, health information technologist on an associate degree, and registered nurse on an associate degree or diploma from an approved programme.

That last one is worth naming plainly, because it is frequently obscured by bachelor's-degree marketing. BLS lists registered nurses as typically entering with a bachelor's degree in nursing, an associate's degree in nursing, or a diploma from an approved nursing program — three routes, one median wage of $93,600. The route affects later mobility more than it affects entry.

The administrative route runs differently: a bachelor's plus prior healthcare experience, with no structured on-the-job training. Which degree does what in that lane is examined in five paths, one title, and the records-and-coding specialisms in roles, pay and credentials.

The support tier is not a waiting room

It is common to read the lower tier as a staging area, and for some people it is: BLS describes healthcare management entrants as often coming from administrative or clinical roles including medical records specialist and administrative assistant.

But the $37,180 median describes a very large number of people for whom it is the job, not a step. Treating an entire occupational group as provisional misreads what the number measures. The honest framing is that the support tier offers fast entry and low barriers, and that movement out of it requires the same licensure or degree that anyone else entering the upper tier needs. Nothing about having worked adjacent to it shortens that.

What the adjacency does buy is different and worth naming precisely. It buys the prior healthcare experience that management roles expect, familiarity with clinical workflow that no classroom supplies, and an employer who may fund part of the training. Those are real advantages. They operate on the cost and the certainty of the transition rather than on its length: the nursing programme is the same length whoever enrols in it.

The corollary matters for anyone weighing the lower tier as a first move. If the plan is to enter fast and cross later, the crossing needs to be scheduled rather than assumed, because the qualification it requires does not accumulate on its own. If the plan is simply to work, the $37,180 figure is the honest expectation, and the fastest route to a better number inside the tier is the setting rather than the title.

Reading a health care job posting

Which tier is this? If the role requires a state licence or a national certification examination, it is in the upper group and the $83,090 median is the relevant benchmark. If not, $37,180 is.

Is the requirement a licence or a preference? "Certification preferred" and "must be licensed in this state" are different statements with different consequences for what you can be paid.

Is this a growth seat or a replacement seat? High-turnover occupations advertise constantly; that is a feature of the occupation, not a signal about the employer.

Which setting is it in? BLS classifies by duties rather than industry, so the same title spans hospitals, offices, outpatient centres, insurers and government — and the setting moves the pay. Where those settings sit relative to each other is mapped in the full salary spread, by setting, and the wider structure of who pays for any of it in how the system actually works.

Questions people actually ask

Are health care jobs well paid?

Two answers, and BLS reports them separately. Healthcare practitioners and technical occupations had a median annual wage of $83,090 in May 2024, above the $49,500 median for all occupations. Healthcare support occupations had a median of $37,180, below it. The gap between the two healthcare tiers is $45,910, which is larger than the entire all-occupations median.

How many health care openings are there each year?

BLS projects about 1.9 million openings each year on average in healthcare occupations from 2024 to 2034, arising from employment growth and the need to replace workers who leave the occupations permanently. Overall employment in these occupations is projected to grow much faster than the average for all occupations.

Which health care job is growing fastest?

Among the roles compared here, the advanced practice nursing group. BLS projects nurse anesthetists, nurse midwives and nurse practitioners to grow 35 per cent from 2024 to 2034, about 134,000 new jobs, against 5 per cent for registered nurses. That is a seven-fold difference in growth rate between two occupations separated by one degree.

What is the fastest way into a health care job?

By entry requirement, medical assistant is the shortest published route: BLS lists a postsecondary nondegree award as typical entry-level education, with 811,000 jobs in 2024 and about 112,300 openings a year. Most states do not require certification, though employers may prefer or require it.

Does a licence make the difference in pay?

It tracks the tier boundary closely. Registered nurses must be licensed and had a May 2024 median of $93,600; advanced practice nurses need a master's, national certification and a state APRN licence, with a median of $132,050. The unlicensed support roles sit in the tier with a $37,180 median.

Which occupation turns over the most?

Of the five compared here, medical assistants: about 112,300 projected annual openings against 811,000 jobs is roughly 13.8 per cent a year. Registered nurses are lowest at about 5.6 per cent, despite having by far the largest headcount at 3,391,000 jobs and 189,100 annual openings.

Do I have to work in a hospital?

No. BLS classifies workers by the duties they perform rather than the industry they perform them in, so the same occupation appears across hospitals, physician offices, outpatient centres, nursing facilities, insurers and government. The setting changes the pay and the hours more than the job title does.
