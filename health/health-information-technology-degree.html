Health Information Technology Degree: What You Are Owed Before Enrolling

This credential is sold overwhelmingly at the associate and certificate level, and federal rules give you more leverage before enrolling than most applicants use. There is a minimum size a programme must meet to be eligible for federal aid at all, and a specific list of things the institution must tell you.

Two numbers anchor the floor: 15 weeks and 600 clock hours for a standard undergraduate programme, or 10 weeks and 300 clock hours for a short one that qualifies only for loans. The single disclosure that decides the most is the transfer of credit policy, including the list of institutions with an articulation agreement.

Who is actually selling this degree

Search the phrase and the results are dominated by community colleges, technical colleges and career institutes. That is not an accident of ranking; it reflects where the credential lives. This is a workforce award, priced and scheduled accordingly.

Which changes the useful question. For a four-year degree the debate is about curriculum and reputation. Here the decisions that matter are shorter and more concrete: how long, how much, what it counts towards, and whether the credits move. All four have federal text behind them.

What counts as a programme at all

34 CFR 668.8 defines an eligible programme as one provided by a participating institution that satisfies the other relevant requirements contained in this section. Those requirements include a floor on size.

Under 668.8(d)(1), an undergraduate programme at a proprietary or vocational institution must require a minimum of 15 weeks of instruction and be at least 600 clock hours, 16 semester or trimester hours, or 24 quarter hours, providing training that prepares a student for gainful employment in a recognised occupation. At a traditional institution, 668.8(c)(3) recognises at least a one-academic-year training program that leads to a certificate, or other nondegree recognized credential.

There is a short-programme tier. Under 668.8(d)(3) a programme must require a minimum of 10 weeks of instruction and be at least 300 clock hours, 8 semester or trimester hours, or 12 quarter hours — and that tier qualifies only for the loan programmes, not the full range of aid. If a course is being sold to you as fundable and it is materially shorter than these floors, the discrepancy is worth resolving before you sign anything.

The list you are entitled to

34 CFR 668.43 sets out institutional information that must be made available. The items that matter here are specific enough to ask for by paragraph.

Cost, at (a)(1): tuition and fees charged to full-time and part-time students, plus estimates of costs for necessary books and supplies and estimates of typical charges for room and board. The refund policy, at (a)(2): any refund policy with which the institution is required to comply for the return of unearned tuition and fees. Withdrawal, at (a)(3): the requirements and procedures for officially withdrawing from the institution.

Then the programme itself, at (a)(5) and (a)(6): the current degree and training programmes, the instructional and laboratory facilities relating to them, the faculty, and the names of associations, agencies or governmental bodies that accredit, approve, or license the institution and its programs. Note the wording — the institution and its programs. Institutional accreditation and programme accreditation are different things, and the disclosure duty covers both.

The disclosure that decides the next four years

34 CFR 668.43(a)(11) requires a description of the transfer of credit policies established by the institution, including a list of institutions with which the institution has established an articulation agreement.

For this credential that single paragraph is worth more than everything else on the page. A technician award is frequently bought as a first step towards an administrator qualification, and whether that step is a step or a detour is decided by named agreements between named institutions.

So ask for the list, not the reassurance. “Our credits generally transfer” is a sentence; an articulation agreement is a document with two institutions’ names on it. The gap between the two is where most of the disappointment in this corner of higher education happens. What the two levels lead to is set out in our guide to the code underneath the name.

The number they already calculate

Under 34 CFR 668.45 an institution annually must prepare the completion or graduation rate of its certificate- or degree-seeking, first-time, full-time undergraduate students, counting those who finish by the end of the 12-month period ending August 31 during which 150 percent of the normal time for completion or graduation from their program has lapsed.

Two things follow. The figure exists whether or not it is offered to you, so asking for it is asking for something that is already on file. And the 150 percent window means a two-year award is measured over three years, which is the honest denominator for a part-time-heavy student body.

There is a second use for that figure. Read alongside the programme length, it tells you what the institution’s own students actually do with a two-year award. If a large share have not finished by the three-year mark, the realistic cost of the credential is higher than the published tuition, because cost scales with terms attended rather than with the nominal length printed in the prospectus.

Reading the answers you get back

Compare cost per credential, not cost per credit hour. A programme with a lower rate and more required hours can be the more expensive one, and the hours figure is on the same page as the aid-eligibility floor.

Treat a refusal or a delay as data. Every item above is either already published or already calculated; an admissions office that cannot produce them quickly is telling you about its record-keeping, which is a poor sign in an institution teaching records management.

And separate the two accreditations in your notes. Institutional accreditation supports federal aid; programme accreditation is what typically governs examination eligibility, and the two can diverge. The way that divergence catches people is set out in the name binds.

What the award leads to

The technician classification prepares people to construct records and clinical databases, control the security and quality of records, and supervise data entry personnel, working under supervision. The senior role it reports into carries a distinct and heavily specified set of duties, described in the duties attached to the chair.

Where the technician award sits among the field’s other job titles, and what each pays, is mapped in roles, pay and credentials and in which code you are in. The terminology itself — what separates the technology from the exchange and the record system — is sorted in HIT, HIE, EHR sorted.

What none of this settles

Federal aid eligibility is not a quality judgement. A programme can clear every floor on this page and still be a poor fit, and a strong programme is not made strong by clearing them.

The disclosures also say nothing about local hiring. Employers in one metropolitan area may ask for a credential that employers three states away never mention, and no federal document will tell you that. Job advertisements in the specific area you intend to work will, and they cost nothing to read.

What the rules do give you is a short list of documents that must exist, on a topic where marketing is otherwise unconstrained. Seven items, all of them already written down somewhere in the institution. Asking for them in one email is the cheapest due diligence available in this decision.

Questions people actually ask

What level is a health information technology degree?

Usually an associate degree or a certificate. The federal programme classification that matches the technician role is CIP 51.0707, Health Information/Medical Records Technology/Technician, whose published definition prepares individuals to work under the supervision of health information administrators. The senior role sits under a different code, so the level of the award matters more than the words in its title.

Is there a minimum size for a programme to be eligible for federal aid?

Yes. Under 34 CFR 668.8(d)(1) a programme at a proprietary or vocational institution must require a minimum of 15 weeks of instruction and be at least 600 clock hours, 16 semester or trimester hours, or 24 quarter hours. A shorter programme under (d)(3) must require a minimum of 10 weeks and be at least 300 clock hours, 8 semester or trimester hours, or 12 quarter hours, and qualifies only for the loan programmes.

What must an institution disclose before I enrol?

34 CFR 668.43 requires institutional information including tuition and fees for full-time and part-time students, estimates of costs for books and supplies and typical room and board charges, any refund policy for the return of unearned tuition and fees, the requirements and procedures for officially withdrawing, the current degree and training programmes and the physical facilities relating to them, the names of the bodies that accredit, approve or license the institution and its programmes, and a description of the transfer of credit policies.

Will an associate degree transfer into a bachelor's programme?

That is an institution-specific question, but the disclosure is required. Under 34 CFR 668.43(a)(11) an institution must provide a description of its transfer of credit policies, including a list of institutions with which it has established an articulation agreement. Ask for that list by name before enrolling rather than after, because a general assurance that credits usually transfer is not the same document.

Is there a published completion rate?

Yes. Under 34 CFR 668.45 an institution annually must prepare the completion or graduation rate of its certificate- or degree-seeking, first-time, full-time undergraduate students, measured by the end of the 12-month period during which 150 percent of the normal time for completion has lapsed. It is an institution-level figure rather than a programme-level one, but it is a published number rather than a claim.

Does the degree itself qualify me for a credential examination?

Not automatically. Examination eligibility runs through programmatic accreditation, which is a separate system from federal aid eligibility and from the programme classification. An institution may be fully eligible for Title IV funds while its programme does not lead to the examination you had in mind, so the accreditation of the specific programme has to be checked on its own.

What is the difference between this and a health information management degree?

Chiefly level and role. The technician classification prepares people to construct records and clinical databases, control security and quality, and supervise data entry personnel, under supervision. The administrator classification prepares people to plan, design and manage the systems and facilities themselves. Both are real occupations; the award level and the accreditation status are what separate the routes.
