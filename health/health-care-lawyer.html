Health Care Lawyer: Which Kind You Actually Need

United States

"Health care lawyer" is not one job. It is a label shared by six practice areas with different clients, different triggers and different fee models. The default meaning — the one the firms ranking for this term are selling — is institutional: the client is a hospital, a practice, an insurer or a vendor.

If you are an individual with a problem, that is usually the wrong lawyer. Two of the most common individual routes cost nothing: a privacy complaint to the HHS Office for Civil Rights, which is free but has a 180-day deadline, and civil legal aid for households at or below 125 per cent of the poverty guidelines. The routing table below is the practical part of this page.

Six jobs, one label

Search results for this term are law firm pages and lawyer directories. Both are optimised to convert a click into a consultation, which means neither has a reason to tell you that the label covers work you almost certainly do not need.

The five statutes that create the work

HHS Office of Inspector General publishes the framework directly, and reading it explains why compliance advice is bought pre-emptively rather than after a problem.

Why one of them changes the whole shape of the advice

The third row is the reason this speciality exists in the form it does. OIG states that for the Physician Self-Referral Law, proof of specific intent to violate the law is not required. The Anti-Kickback Statute, by contrast, requires a knowing and wilful violation.

On identical facts, then, an arrangement can be clean under one statute and a violation of the other. Good faith is a defence to one and irrelevant to the other. That asymmetry is why provider organisations pay for structural review of referral and compensation arrangements before signing rather than opinions afterwards — and it is why this work is billed as prevention.

It also explains something a patient might otherwise misread. When a hospital declines to do something that looks obviously helpful — waiving a charge, arranging transport, paying a physician differently — the constraint is frequently one of these statutes rather than indifference.

If you are an individual, route by problem

The single most useful move is to stop searching by profession and start searching by the specific problem, because the routes differ in cost by orders of magnitude.

The free route with a deadline on it

Privacy complaints go to the HHS Office for Civil Rights and cost nothing. Three filing routes exist: the OCR Complaint Portal at ocrportal.hhs.gov; email to OCRComplaint@hhs.gov, which OCR itself flags as carrying an interception risk for personally identifiable information; and post to Centralized Case Management Operations, US Department of Health and Human Services, 200 Independence Avenue SW, Room 509F HHH Building, Washington DC 20201.

The constraint people miss is the clock. A complaint must be filed within 180 days of when you knew that the act or omission occurred, though OCR may extend that period if you show good cause. OCR also takes complaints about substance use disorder treatment confidentiality under 42 CFR Part 2.

Because the deadline runs from knowledge rather than from the event, the practical advice is to date your own discovery in writing at the moment it happens. Your right to obtain the underlying records in the first place is covered in our guide to patient portals and your right of access.

When you cannot pay

The Legal Services Corporation was created by Congress in 1974 and is the largest funder of civil legal aid in the United States. It funds 129 independent legal aid organisations running more than 800 offices across every state and territory, and distributes nearly 94 per cent of its congressional appropriation to them.

Eligibility is set at 125 per cent or below the federal poverty guidelines. The subject matter — housing, family, employment, consumer, benefits — is precisely where the consequences of a medical event land, which makes legal aid a more realistic first call than a health care firm for most individuals.

With about 31,500 openings a year against 864,800 jobs — roughly 3.6 per cent of the occupation annually — entry is competitive and hourly rates at the top end are set accordingly. That economics is why individual-side work concentrates in contingency fees and legal aid rather than hourly retainers.

Verifying that someone is actually admitted

Anyone practising law must have earned a law degree and, as BLS puts it, passed a licensing examination known as the bar exam and been admitted to the bar by their state's highest court. Two consequences follow that are worth acting on.

First, admission is state-specific. A lawyer admitted elsewhere is not automatically able to act for you where you live, and "national" branding on a directory listing does not change that.

Second, admission is a matter of public record held by the state's bar authority, so it is checkable in a couple of minutes before any money changes hands. Directory profiles, award badges and rankings are marketing artefacts and are not the same thing as a licence.

Where this connects

Two adjacent questions decide the answer for most individuals before a lawyer is involved at all. Whether your plan is a licensed insurer or a self-funded employer arrangement determines both the appeal route and the regulator that hears a complaint, which is set out in who pays your claim. And if cost is the underlying problem, federally funded health centres apply a fixed discount schedule rather than a case-by-case decision — see how the section 330 programme works. The wider structure sits in how US health care actually works.

Questions people actually ask

What does a health care lawyer actually do?

In its default sense the client is an institution, not a patient. The work is regulatory: advising hospitals, practices, insurers and vendors on the federal fraud and abuse laws, on privacy rules, on payer contracts and on transactions. Patient-side work exists but is usually carried out by lawyers who describe themselves by the specific problem, such as medical malpractice or disability benefits.

Which laws create most of this work?

HHS Office of Inspector General identifies five: the False Claims Act at 31 U.S.C. 3729–3733, the Anti-Kickback Statute at 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b), the Physician Self-Referral or Stark Law at 42 U.S.C. 1395nn, the Exclusion Statute at 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7, and the Civil Monetary Penalties Law at 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7a. Together they explain why compliance advice is bought before anything goes wrong.

Why is the Stark Law treated differently from the others?

Because it is strict liability. OIG states that for the Physician Self-Referral Law, proof of specific intent to violate the law is not required. The Anti-Kickback Statute by contrast requires a knowing and wilful violation. That difference is why an arrangement can be lawful under one and unlawful under the other on identical facts.

I am a patient with a complaint. Do I need a health care lawyer?

Usually not the institutional kind. Route by the problem instead. A privacy breach goes to the HHS Office for Civil Rights, free of charge, within 180 days. A benefits or claim denial goes through your plan's appeal route first, which depends on whether your plan is insured or self-funded. Injury claims go to a medical malpractice attorney, who works on a different fee model entirely.

How do I file a HIPAA complaint, and does it cost anything?

It is free. File with the HHS Office for Civil Rights through the OCR Complaint Portal at ocrportal.hhs.gov, by email to OCRComplaint@hhs.gov, or by post to Centralized Case Management Operations, US Department of Health and Human Services, 200 Independence Avenue SW, Room 509F HHH Building, Washington DC 20201. The complaint must be filed within 180 days of when you knew the act or omission occurred, though OCR may extend that for good cause.

What if I cannot afford a lawyer?

The Legal Services Corporation funds 129 independent legal aid organisations operating more than 800 offices nationwide, serving people at 125 per cent or below the federal poverty guidelines. It is the largest funder of civil legal aid in the United States and has been since Congress created it in 1974. Civil legal aid covers benefits, housing and consumer matters, which is where most health-related legal problems actually sit.

What do lawyers earn, and why does that matter to me?

The BLS median annual wage for lawyers was $151,160 in May 2024, with the lowest ten per cent under $72,780 and the highest ten per cent over $239,200. It matters because a practice economics of that shape explains why institution-side work is abundant and individual-side work is concentrated in contingency-fee and legal aid models rather than hourly retainers.
