Health Care Agency: What Certification Actually Buys

Last verified
9 Aug 2026
Jurisdiction
United States
Data sources

The useful fact about a home health agency is not its marketing but whether it is Medicare-certified, because certification is what produces published evidence about it. Certified agencies appear on Care Compare with two star ratings drawn from different data.

And there is a trap in that evidence nobody mentions. An agency needs at least 20 complete quality episodes per measure and reportable data for 5 of 7 measures to get a quality rating, and 40 or more completed surveys to get a patient survey rating. Below those volumes it shows no stars — which is not the same as showing bad ones.

One phrase, two unrelated things

The results for this search mix directory sites, a state government page and individual home care companies. That mix is not noise. The word agency is doing two jobs.

In its administrative sense it means a government body — a state or county health department. In its commercial sense it means a home care or home health business. Someone searching for the first finds the second, and the reverse, because a search box cannot tell them apart.

This page is about the second, because that is the one where a decision usually has to be made and where the published evidence is easy to misread. It does not name, rate or recommend any agency.

What a home health agency is, in CMS terms

CMS describes a home health agency as an agency or organization which: Is primarily engaged in providing skilled nursing services and other therapeutic services, with established policies, physician or nurse supervision, clinical records, state licensing where applicable, and which meets the federal requirements in the interest of the health and safety of individuals who are furnished services by the HHA.

The governing rules sit in 42 CFR Part 484 and section 1891 of the Social Security Act, and CMS notes that the agency survey is conducted in accordance with the appropriate protocols and substantive requirements in the statute and regulations.

Two words in that description carry the weight. Skilled distinguishes this from companionship and personal care services, which are a different market with different rules. And survey means an inspection regime exists, which is the mechanism that generates everything in the next sections.

Why "provider" is the operative classification

Under 42 CFR 400.202, provider is a defined list of institutions, and home health agencies are on it — alongside hospitals, critical access hospitals, skilled nursing facilities and hospices. A physician, by contrast, is a supplier.

That placement is not a technicality. Being in the provider list is what puts an organisation inside the certification and survey regime rather than outside it, and it is why federal quality data exists for home health agencies and not for every business that sells care at home. Why the two words are kept apart across federal rulebooks is set out in a legal term, not a synonym.

The practical version: an agency that is not Medicare-certified is not necessarily bad, but it sits outside the system that produces comparable public evidence, so nothing in the rest of this page will be available for it.

Two ratings, two different data sources

CMS publishes two star ratings for home health agencies, and they are built from unrelated inputs. Reading them as one number is the commonest mistake made with this data.

An agency can score well on one and poorly on the other without contradiction, because one is measuring recorded outcomes and the other is measuring how the service felt to the people receiving it. Both are legitimate; neither substitutes for the other.

The seven measures, named

The Quality of Patient Care rating is built from seven measures: timely initiation of care, improvement in ambulation, improvement in bed transferring, improvement in bathing, improvement in shortness of breath, improvement in oral medication management, and home health within-stay potentially preventable hospitalisation.

Read that list for what it is and is not. Six of the seven are about a patient getting better at a specific everyday function, and one is about avoiding a hospital admission during the episode. None of them measures kindness, reliability of scheduling, or continuity of the same carer — which are the things families most often care about. Those belong to the survey rating, which is precisely why the two exist separately.

The threshold nobody mentions

Here is the part that changes how you read a comparison page.

To receive a Quality of Patient Care rating, an agency needs at least 20 complete quality episodes for each measure and reportable data for 5 of the 7 measures. To receive a Patient Survey rating it needs 40 or more completed surveys over the four-quarter reporting period.

Both are volume thresholds. They exist so that a rating is not computed from a handful of cases, which is statistically sound and entirely reasonable. But the consequence is systematic: small and new agencies are disproportionately unrated, regardless of how they perform.

So a comparison view is not a ranking of all agencies in your area. It is a ranking of the ones large enough to clear a volume bar, with everything below it shown blank. That is a selection effect built into the display, and it is invisible unless you know the numbers.

What an absent rating actually means

An absent rating is an absence of evidence. It is not evidence of absence of quality, and it is not a low score.

The practical response is to switch instruments rather than to eliminate the agency. If the stars are blank, the questions that do the work are the ones you ask directly: how long the agency has operated, how many patients it currently serves, whether it is Medicare-certified at all, when it was last surveyed, and how continuity of carer is handled. None of those require the rating system.

The reverse caution also applies. A rated agency is a rated agency, not a verified one for your situation: the quality rating measures functional improvement across its whole caseload, which may have little to do with the specific needs in front of you.

There is a subtler version of the same trap inside the rated group. Because the quality rating requires reportable data for only five of the seven measures, two agencies can both hold a rating while having been scored on different measures. The star that appears on the screen is a single glyph either way, and nothing in that glyph tells you which five were counted. When the difference between two agencies is small, that is a reason to treat the comparison as a starting point rather than a verdict.

The other sense, briefly

Where the phrase means a government body, the questions are different. A state or county health agency is a regulator and a public health function rather than a service you purchase, and complaints about a licensed provider generally route to a state agency rather than to a federal one.

Knowing which sense you need before you search saves the commonest wasted step here, which is contacting a company about a regulatory question or a regulator about a service question. How to route a health problem to the body that actually decides it is set out in route before you search, and the wider map of who owes what in which door, and what it must do.

A verification order

A last framing note. Every instrument described here measures the agency, not the visit. Certification says the organisation met federal conditions; the star ratings say something about its caseload over a reporting period. Neither is a statement about the individual carer who will actually arrive at the door, how often the same person comes back, or how the schedule holds together in a difficult week. Those are the variables families report caring about most, and no federal dataset captures them, which is a limit worth naming rather than working around.

First: is it Medicare-certified? If not, none of the federal evidence exists for it, and that is the single most consequential fact about your ability to check anything.

Second: are there stars, and are there two of them? Look at both ratings separately and note which is missing.

Third: if a rating is blank, is the agency small or new? That is the usual reason, and it is answerable by asking.

Fourth: what will you actually be billed? Certification governs quality reporting, not your cost, and coverage depends on your plan and on whether the services are skilled. Questions people actually ask

What is a home health agency?

CMS describes a home health agency as an agency or organization which is primarily engaged in providing skilled nursing services and other therapeutic services, with established policies, physician or nurse supervision, clinical records, state licensing, and compliance with federal health and safety requirements. The applicable rules sit in 42 CFR Part 484 and section 1891 of the Social Security Act.

Why does it matter that an agency is a provider?

Because 42 CFR 400.202 defines provider as a list of institutions that includes home health agencies, while a physician is defined as a supplier. Being in the provider list is what places a home health agency inside the certification and survey regime rather than outside it, and it is why federal quality data exists for these organisations at all.

What star ratings does Medicare publish for home health?

Two, and they measure different things. The Quality of Patient Care Star Rating is built from OASIS assessments and Medicare claims data. The Patient Survey Star Rating comes from the Home Health CAHPS survey. Both appear on the Care Compare website at Medicare.gov.

What are the thresholds for getting a rating?

For the Quality of Patient Care rating, an agency needs at least 20 complete quality episodes for each measure and reportable data for 5 of the 7 measures. For the Patient Survey rating it needs 40 or more completed surveys over the four-quarter reporting period. Agencies below those thresholds do not receive that rating.

Does no rating mean a bad agency?

No. It most often means the agency is small or new, because the thresholds are volume thresholds rather than quality thresholds. An absent rating is an absence of evidence and should be read as a prompt to ask more questions directly, not as a negative finding.

Which measures feed the quality rating?

Seven: timely initiation of care, improvement in ambulation, improvement in bed transferring, improvement in bathing, improvement in shortness of breath, improvement in oral medication management, and home health within-stay potentially preventable hospitalisation. Five of the seven must be reportable for the agency to receive a rating.

What else does health care agency mean?

A government body. State health departments and county health agencies use the word in its administrative sense, and several results for this phrase are state pages. That sense has no connection to home care, and the two are easy to conflate because both appear for the same query.
