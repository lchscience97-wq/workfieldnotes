Health Care Administration Salary: The Full Spread, by Setting

Last verified
3 Aug 2026
Jurisdiction
United States
Wage data
BLS, May 2024
Sources
5 cited
Reading time
9 min

The median annual wage for health care administration — the occupation BLS calls medical and health services managers — was $117,960 in May 2024, or $56.71 an hour.

The median is the least useful number on this page. The lowest ten per cent earned under $69,680 and the highest ten per cent earned more than $219,080: a spread of about $149,400, wider than the median itself. Where you land inside that spread is decided mostly by setting and seniority, and both are choosable.

The range, not the median

Salary pages for this field almost always lead with $117,960 and stop. That single figure hides the structure of the occupation.

Note the asymmetry. The distance from the median up to the ninetieth percentile ($101,120) is more than twice the distance down to the tenth ($48,280). This is a right-skewed distribution: the ceiling is far away, the floor is close, and the average therefore sits above the median. When a page quotes an "average" for this field it is quoting a number that a minority of the occupation earns.

Setting moves the number more than tenure does

This is the finding worth acting on, and it rarely appears with the employment share attached.

The gap between the highest and lowest of the top five settings is $33,370 at the median — roughly a third of an entry-level salary, available by changing employer type rather than by waiting.

Two qualifications. Government is the best-paying setting but employs only 7 per cent of the occupation, so the opening rate is low. Hospitals pay nearly as much and employ 29 per cent, which makes them the realistic target for most people optimising on pay.

The five settings above account for about 66 per cent of the occupation. The remaining third — insurers, consultancies, pharmaceutical companies, health IT vendors, home health services — is not broken out by BLS in the top-industry table, so no reliable median is published for it. Anyone quoting a precise figure for "payer-side healthcare administration salary" is not getting it from BLS.

The useful comparison is not against all occupations — almost any management role wins that. It is against the management jobs a similar person could plausibly hold.

What a master's actually buys

BLS lists a bachelor's degree as the typical entry-level education for this occupation, with less than five years of related work experience and no on-the-job training. A master's is not an entry requirement.

Where it matters is higher up. BLS states that advancement to top-level executive positions may require a master's degree. And the field's principal credential, the FACHE, requires a master's or other post-baccalaureate degree as a precondition — alongside five years of healthcare management experience and a currently held executive position.

So the honest framing is not "a master's raises your salary by X". It is that the upper portion of the $219,080 tail is effectively gated by one, and the lower two-thirds of the distribution is not. Whether that gate is worth the tuition depends on whether you intend to pass through it.

Employment growth is not wage growth

BLS projects employment to grow 23 per cent between 2024 and 2034, from 616,200 jobs to a projected 759,100 — an increase of 142,900, with about 62,100 openings a year. For comparison, other management occupations are projected at 6 per cent and all occupations at 3 per cent.

BLS does not publish wage projections. Strong employment growth is frequently presented on salary pages as though it forecasts rising pay; it does not. It forecasts more positions. Whether pay rises depends on whether the supply of qualified candidates grows faster or slower than those positions, which nobody publishes a number for.

Why aggregator figures differ from BLS

Search this term and you will find numbers ranging from roughly $60,000 to well over $150,000 for what appears to be the same job. The spread is not error. It is four different measurement choices.

Two practical rules follow. First, check which occupation a figure is attached to before comparing it — a page quoting $67,310 or $50,250 is describing a different occupation entirely, not a junior version of this one. Second, treat any figure without a stated month and year as unusable for comparison, because BLS itself revises annually.

What is actually negotiable

Within a posted band, three things move and two rarely do.

Moves: the starting point inside the band, which is set by how the employer scores your prior scope rather than your years; the title, which determines the band you are compared against at the next review; and the review timing, which can be pulled forward to six months where the band's midpoint is out of reach at hire.

Rarely moves: the band itself, which in hospital and government settings is usually fixed by a compensation structure that the hiring manager does not control; and licensure reimbursement in settings where it is already standard.

The scope point deserves emphasis because it is where the $149,400 spread is actually decided. Two candidates with identical tenure can be scored very differently on budget size, headcount and number of sites — and those three numbers, not the years, are what the compensation structure keys on. Anyone preparing for this conversation should arrive able to state all three from their last role without looking them up.

What the figure excludes

Three things sit outside the wage estimate and are worth pricing yourself.

Hours. Most administrators work full time and some work more than 40 hours a week. Evening and weekend work is common in settings that run around the clock, and on-call availability for emergencies is normal in hospital and nursing home roles. The hourly median of $56.71 assumes a standard year.

Licensure cost. Nursing home administration requires a state licence in every state, with its own examination, fees and renewal cycle. That is a real, recurring cost attached to one of the lower-paying settings.

Physical risk. BLS notes that medical and health services managers have one of the highest rates of injuries and illnesses of all occupations, from incidents such as slips, falls and overexertion — a consequence of working inside an operating clinical facility rather than a detached office.

The role these figures describe, and the licence rule behind them, are set out in our guide to what a health care administrator does.

What the first five years look like

The distribution above is a snapshot of everyone in the occupation at once, which makes it hard to read as a trajectory. Three anchors help.

Entry sits near the tenth percentile, not the median. A first administrative role with a bachelor's degree and no prior management scope lands closer to the $69,680 end than to $117,960. The median describes someone with real budget and headcount responsibility, which BLS reflects in listing "less than five years" of related work experience as typical for entry — that experience is a precondition, not something accumulated in the job.

The move that matters is scope, not tenure. Because compensation structures key on budget size, headcount and number of sites, a lateral move that doubles any of those three does more for pay than two years of good performance in a fixed role. This is why people in this field change employers more often than the job security of healthcare would suggest.

The setting decision compounds. Starting in nursing and residential care rather than a hospital costs about $31,440 at the median, and it also anchors the band you are benchmarked against in the next negotiation. Moving between settings gets harder as titles become setting-specific, so the choice made at entry has a longer tail than it appears to.

None of the above is published as a career-path statistic; BLS reports the occupation as a whole, not by year of experience. These are readings of the structure of the published data, and they should be treated as that rather than as measured trajectories.

Questions people actually ask

What is the average health care administration salary?

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported a median annual wage of $117,960 for medical and health services managers in May 2024, equivalent to $56.71 an hour. Median is more informative than average here because the top of the range pulls the average upward.

What is the salary range in health care administration?

In May 2024 the lowest ten per cent earned less than $69,680 and the highest ten per cent earned more than $219,080. That is a spread of about $149,400 between the two ends, which is wider than the entire median wage.

Which health care setting pays administrators the most?

Government, excluding state and local education and hospitals, at a median of $132,620 in May 2024. Hospitals followed at $130,690. The lowest of the top industries was nursing and residential care facilities at $99,250.

Does health care administration pay more than other management jobs?

More than the management average, less than the technical management specialisms. In May 2024 the median for other management occupations was $104,030 against $117,960 for medical and health services managers, but computer and information systems managers were at $171,200 and financial managers at $161,700.

Do you need a master's degree to earn a higher salary in health care administration?

Not to enter. BLS lists a bachelor's degree as the typical entry-level education. However, it also notes that advancement to top executive positions may require a master's, and the FACHE credential requires a post-baccalaureate degree, so the upper end of the range is effectively gated by one.

Is health care administration salary growing?

Employment is growing sharply. BLS projects 23 per cent growth from 2024 to 2034 with about 62,100 openings a year. BLS does not publish wage projections, so growth in employment should not be read as a forecast of wage growth.

How much does a health care administrator make per hour?

The median works out to $56.71 an hour in May 2024. Most of these roles are salaried rather than hourly, and the figure excludes on-call and evening commitments common in facilities that operate around the clock.
