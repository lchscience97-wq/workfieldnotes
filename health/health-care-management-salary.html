Health Care Management Salary: Which Code Are You In?

Last verified
8 Aug 2026
Jurisdiction
United States
Wage data
BLS, May 2024; SOC 2018
Sources
5 cited
Reading time
9 min

Before asking what health care management pays, work out which occupation you are in. The federal survey covers about 830 occupations and none of them is called health care management. Three codes compete for the same job titles, with May 2024 medians of $117,960, $108,390 and $102,950.

That is a $15,010 spread produced by classification alone, before experience or employer. And the assignment is not made by your job title: under the SOC, workers are classified into occupations based on their job duties, not their job titles.

What the pages ranking here are doing

The results split between salary aggregators and university programme pages. Both quote a number. Neither states which occupation code the number belongs to, and that omission is the entire problem, because the same job description can sit in three codes with three different medians.

This page is not another salary figure. It is the mapping step that has to happen before a salary figure means anything. If what you want is the distribution by workplace — hospital against nursing facility against government — that is a different cut of the same data, set out in the full spread, by setting.

The rule that decides everything

The federal wage programme states its own classification rule plainly: Under the SOC, workers are classified into occupations based on their job duties, not their job titles. The survey produces estimates for approximately 830 occupations across 23 major groups.

Two consequences follow immediately, and they are the ones salary pages skip.

Your badge does not determine your code. Two people both called "healthcare operations manager" can be counted in different occupations if one plans clinical service delivery and the other runs facilities and records.

The reverse is also true. Two people with quite different titles can be in the same code, and therefore behind the same median, if their duties match.

So the honest first question is not "what does this title pay" but "which of the 830 does this work belong to". Everything below is the answer to that.

How to make the assignment yourself

Since the classification runs on duties, the assignment is something you can reason about from a job description rather than something you have to look up. Three questions do most of the work.

Is the subject matter of the decisions clinical? If the role plans, directs or coordinates the delivery of health services — service lines, clinical staffing, quality, compliance with care standards — it belongs with medical and health services managers, and $117,960 is the relevant median. The health-specific content of the decisions is what puts it there, not the fact that the employer is a hospital.

Or is it the building and the back office? Facilities, maintenance, records administration, mail, procurement and general office operations point to administrative services and facilities managers. Those functions exist identically in a bank and in a clinic, which is exactly why they are classified together across industries rather than under health care.

Or is it general operational responsibility? Where a role owns profit-and-loss and day-to-day operations without the health-specific content, general and operations managers is the closer fit, at $102,950.

The common error is to reason from the employer. Working at a hospital does not place you in the health services managers code; managing health services does. A facilities director at a hospital and a facilities director at a warehouse are doing the same classified work, and the survey treats them accordingly.

The spread inside one code dwarfs the spread between them

Here is the number that puts the classification question in proportion. Within medical and health services managers, the May 2024 median was $117,960, the lowest 10 per cent earned less than $69,680, and the highest 10 per cent earned more than $219,080.

That is a $149,400 span from tail to tail, against a $15,010 gap between the three occupation codes. The internal range is roughly 10 times the between-code range.

Read together, the two facts say something specific. Getting the code right tells you which distribution you are in; it does not tell you where in that distribution you sit, and the second question is worth about ten times as much as the first. Which is why a page quoting a single median — any median — is answering the smaller of the two questions.

Who is missing from every figure on this page

The federal survey covers all part-time and full-time wage and salary employees and excludes self-employed individuals, owners and partners in unincorporated firms, household workers and unpaid family members.

For this field that exclusion removes an identifiable group: independent practice managers, owner-operators of small clinical businesses, and partners in advisory firms. None of them are in any number quoted here or on any competing page that cites BLS. If your intended path is ownership rather than employment, the federal series does not describe your outcome, and no amount of reading it more carefully will make it do so.

The comparison problem this creates is worth naming, because people attempt it constantly. An employed manager at $117,960 works alongside an employer carrying the other half of payroll taxes, benefits, paid leave and every unbilled hour. An owner's draw is gross revenue out of which all of those come from the same figure. Setting the two side by side without adjusting for that difference overstates the independent route, and there is no federal series that will correct the comparison for you. The version of this problem that arises for advisory work specifically is set out in the job BLS does not measure.

Mean or median, and why it matters here

The programme publishes both mean wages and median wages, with reference periods aligned to May of each year.

For a right-skewed distribution the two are not interchangeable. In the medical and health services managers figures, the distance from the median up to the ninetieth percentile is $101,120 while the distance down to the tenth is $48,280 — the upper tail is about 2.1 times as long. A mean computed over that shape sits above the median and describes fewer people than it appears to.

So when two sources disagree, the first question is which occupation code, and the second is mean or median. Those two together account for most of the discrepancy people notice between a university prospectus and a federal table.

Where these numbers sit in the sector

All three management codes sit well above the sector they operate in. BLS reports a May 2024 median of $83,090 for healthcare practitioners and technical occupations and $37,180 for healthcare support occupations, against $49,500 for all occupations.

Set the lowest of the three management figures, $102,950, against the practitioner tier at $83,090 and the difference is $19,860. Set it against the support tier and the difference is $65,770. The management layer is paid above the clinical layer it coordinates, which is a structural feature of the sector rather than a local anomaly — and the two-tier split underneath it is examined in two tiers, $45,910 apart.

Using this before a negotiation or an enrolment

Name the code before the number. Write down which of the three the role's duties put it in, then use that occupation's distribution rather than a blended figure.

Ask which percentile, not which median. Inside one code the span is $149,400. A conversation about where in the range a role sits is worth ten times a conversation about which median to quote.

Check whether the figure is a mean. If a source does not say, treat the number as unlocated.

Check the employment model. If the plan involves ownership or independent practice, none of these figures apply to it. What the various qualifications actually buy before any of this becomes relevant is in five paths, one title, with the job structure itself in where the administration jobs are and the day-to-day content in what a health care administrator does.

Questions people actually ask

What does health care management pay?

It depends which occupation the job is classified in, because there is no federal occupation called health care management. The three plausible codes had May 2024 medians of $117,960 for medical and health services managers, $108,390 for administrative services managers and $102,950 for general and operations managers. The spread between the highest and lowest is $15,010.

Why do salary sites give different numbers for the same job?

Three reasons compound. Different sources map the same job title to different occupation codes. BLS classifies workers by job duties rather than job titles, so the title on your badge does not determine the code. And the federal survey reports both mean and median wages, which differ because these distributions are right-skewed.

How do I tell which code my job is in?

By duties, not by title. Under the SOC, workers are classified into occupations based on their job duties, not their job titles. If the work is planning and directing clinical or health services delivery, it points to medical and health services managers. If it is facilities, records and general office operations, it points to administrative services managers.

Is the self-employed manager included in these figures?

No. The federal wage survey covers all part-time and full-time wage and salary workers and explicitly excludes the self-employed, owners and partners in unincorporated firms, household workers and unpaid family workers. Any figure describing an independent consultant or practice owner is coming from somewhere other than the federal series.

How wide is the range inside one code?

Very wide. For medical and health services managers the May 2024 median was $117,960, with the lowest 10 per cent under $69,680 and the highest 10 per cent over $219,080. That is a $149,400 span, which is more than nine times the $15,010 gap between the three occupation codes.

Does moving into executive work change the number?

Substantially. BLS reports a May 2024 median of $206,420 for chief executives against $102,950 for general and operations managers, a difference of $103,470 inside the same broad top executives group. That group had a median of $105,350 overall, which shows how far the chief executive figure sits above the rest of it.

How many occupations does the survey actually cover?

About 830, organised into 23 major occupational groups under the Standard Occupational Classification. Health care management is not one of them. Every published figure for it is therefore a figure for some other, formally defined occupation, whether or not the page quoting it says so.
