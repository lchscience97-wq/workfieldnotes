Health Care Technology Jobs: Six Occupations, One Search Box

United States, federal

Every result for this phrase is a job board, and that is the finding. "Health care technology" is a category boards created to group postings. It is not an occupation, and no federal wage figure attaches to it.

What sits underneath is at least six distinct BLS occupations, with May 2024 medians running from $50,250 to $117,960 — a spread of $67,710 inside one search box. Different entry requirements, different growth, different hiring volumes. This page sorts them.

A shelf, not a job

Job boards need categories, and categories have to be broad enough to hold inventory. Health care technology is a good shelf: it captures anything technical happening near medicine, which is a lot of postings.

It is a poor unit for a career decision, because the things on the shelf are not variants of each other. A person repairing an infusion pump, a person designing one, a person coding a chart and a person running a service line are in four occupations that share no entry requirement and no wage band.

Our guide to the two regulators behind the phrase covers what the products are. This page covers what the jobs are.

The device side

Two occupations sit closest to physical equipment, and they are far apart.

Medical equipment repairers had a median annual wage of $62,630 in May 2024, equivalent to $30.11 an hour, with 68,000 jobs in 2024, projected growth of 13 percent over 2024–34 adding 8,800 positions, and about 7,300 openings a year. BLS gives an associate's degree as the typical entry level, noting that some enter with a high school diploma and relevant training or work experience.

Bioengineers and biomedical engineers had a median of $106,950, or $51.42 an hour, with 22,200 jobs, projected growth of 5 percent and about 1,300 openings a year. The entry level is a bachelor's degree.

Hold those two openings figures next to each other. The repair occupation generates roughly 5.6 times as many vacancies a year as the engineering one, at a lower entry requirement. High pay and high hiring are not the same signal, and the phrase on the shelf hides which one a posting represents.

The records side

Medical records specialists had a median annual wage of $50,250 in May 2024, with 194,800 jobs, projected growth of 7 percent and about 14,200 openings a year. BLS lists the typical entry level as a postsecondary nondegree award.

This is the occupation most often mislabelled in postings, because the work is genuinely technical — classification systems, health data standards, release of information — while the federal entry level sits below an associate degree. Our guides to which code you are actually in and the degree where the name binds deal with the credential side of that confusion.

The general IT side, sitting in a hospital

A large share of postings on this shelf are ordinary information technology roles whose employer happens to be a health system. BLS reports by occupation, not employer, so the applicable figures are the IT ones.

Computer and information technology occupations as a group had a median annual wage of $105,990 in May 2024, with about 317,700 openings a year across the group.

But the group median conceals a wide floor. Computer support specialists had a combined median of $61,550 across 882,300 jobs in 2024 — and the two halves of that occupation are not alike. Network support specialists had a median of $73,340; user support specialists, who make up the large majority of the headcount, had $60,340.

That composition is why the combined figure sits so close to the lower of the two. An occupation's median is a headcount-weighted number, so when one component dominates the population it dominates the median, and the better-paid component barely moves it. Anyone reading $61,550 as the typical support wage is reading the user support figure with a rounding error attached.

Their projection is the most instructive number in this article. BLS projects a 3 percent decline over 2024–34, a loss of 24,200 jobs, while still expecting about 50,500 openings a year, primarily from worker replacement and retirement. BLS attributes the decline to automation tools such as chatbots for user support and to outsourcing for network support.

Declining and hiring at the same time

That combination confuses more readers than any other feature of projection data, so it is worth stating plainly.

Employment change measures the size of the occupation at the end of the decade. Openings measure how many positions need filling each year, which is dominated by people leaving. An occupation can shrink and still advertise heavily, because most vacancies are replacements rather than additions.

The practical reading: a declining occupation with large openings is accessible now and less certain later. A growing occupation with small openings, like biomedical engineering, is the reverse. Neither is better; they are different bets, and the shelf label tells you nothing about which one you are looking at.

Set against the all-occupations median of $49,500 in May 2024, every row here is above it. That is the honest case for the shelf: the work pays. What the shelf cannot tell you is which row you would be joining.

Two comparisons in the table are worth a second look because they cut against intuition. Biomedical engineering at $106,950 and the computer and IT group at $105,990 sit within about a thousand dollars of each other, despite one requiring a specialised engineering degree and the other being a broad family of occupations. And medical equipment repair at $62,630 sits just above computer support at $61,550, despite the first being hands-on work on regulated devices and the second being the entry rung of a much larger field. Where wages converge like that, the differentiator is rarely pay; it is how many doors the occupation opens next, and how many vacancies exist to walk through in the first place.

Sorting a posting in one minute

Read the duties, not the title. Repairing, calibrating or servicing equipment is the device occupation. Designing or testing it is engineering. Coding, abstracting, auditing or releasing records is the records occupation. Running a department, a service line or a budget is management. Supporting users, networks or systems is an IT occupation whose employer happens to be a hospital.

Then check the stated entry requirement against the occupation you identified. A posting demanding a bachelor's degree for duties BLS places at a postsecondary nondegree level is an employer preference, not a field requirement, and it is negotiable in a way a licensure requirement is not.

One further check is worth thirty seconds. Ask which department the role reports into, because it usually settles the occupation faster than the duties list does. Clinical engineering and biomedical services departments hold the device roles. Health information management holds the records roles. Information services or IT holds the support and infrastructure roles. Administration holds the management roles. Organisational charts are less imaginative than job titles, and that is exactly what makes them useful here.

Where a posting genuinely spans two of these — and some do, particularly at smaller employers — the wage benchmark should be the higher-requirement occupation, because that is the work the employer is buying. A role that repairs equipment and also administers the asset management system is not two half-jobs; it is a device role with a systems component, and the comparison set is device roles.

What the shelf cannot tell you

It cannot tell you the wage, because it spans $67,710 of median. It cannot tell you the entry requirement, because the six occupations run from a nondegree award to a bachelor's degree. It cannot tell you whether hiring is strong, because openings per year in this table range from about 1,300 to about 317,700.

And it cannot tell you the direction of travel, because one of these occupations is projected to shrink while another grows 23 percent. Questions people actually ask

Is health care technology a BLS occupation?

No. It is a job-board category. Postings collected under it map to several distinct federal occupations with different entry requirements and very different pay, from medical records specialists at a $50,250 median in May 2024 to medical and health services managers at $117,960. The first job of anyone reading these postings is working out which occupation a given one actually is.

What does the device side pay?

Medical equipment repairers had a median annual wage of $62,630 in May 2024, with 68,000 jobs, projected growth of 13 percent over 2024–34 adding 8,800 positions, and about 7,300 openings a year. BLS lists an associate's degree as the typical entry level, though some enter with a high school diploma and relevant training or work experience.

What about engineering roles?

Bioengineers and biomedical engineers had a median annual wage of $106,950 in May 2024, with 22,200 jobs and projected growth of 5 percent over 2024–34. The entry level is a bachelor's degree. Note the scale: about 1,300 openings a year, which is a small occupation relative to the attention it receives.

Do general IT wages apply in a hospital?

The occupational medians do, because BLS reports by occupation rather than by employer. Computer and information technology occupations as a group had a median annual wage of $105,990 in May 2024 with about 317,700 openings a year. But the specific occupation matters far more than the group: computer support specialists had a combined median of $61,550.

Is support work growing?

Employment is not, but hiring is. BLS projects computer support specialists to decline 3 percent over 2024–34, a loss of 24,200 jobs, while still generating about 50,500 openings a year, primarily from worker replacement and retirement. Falling employment and steady hiring are not contradictory, and conflating them is the commonest misreading of projection data.

Which occupation pays most in this group?

Medical and health services managers, at a median annual wage of $117,960 in May 2024, with 616,200 jobs, projected growth of 23 percent and about 62,100 openings a year. The gap between that and medical records specialists at $50,250 is $67,710, and both can appear under the same search phrase.

How do I tell which occupation a posting is?

Read the duties rather than the title. Repairing or calibrating equipment is the device occupation. Designing it is engineering. Coding, abstracting or releasing records is the records occupation. Running a service line or a department is management. Supporting users or a network is an IT occupation that happens to sit in a hospital. The title is marketing; the duties determine which federal wage figure applies.
