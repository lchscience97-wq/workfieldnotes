Health Information Management Degree: The Name Binds

Last verified
11 Aug 2026
Jurisdiction
United States
Data sources

This is the one degree in the field where the exact name on the programme is load-bearing. AHIMA lists completing a CAHIIM-accredited HIM programme as the pathway to the RHIA and RHIT exams — baccalaureate level for RHIA, associate level for RHIT.

And the exclusion is explicit. AHIMA states it only accepts HIM degree programs to sit for the RHIA, and that health informatics is not currently supported. Two programme names that read as synonyms lead to different places. Check the name and the accreditation before paying.

Why this one is different

The results here are community colleges, state universities and a discussion forum. Same shape as every other degree query on this site — and this time the underlying answer is the opposite of the one those pages usually deserve.

Elsewhere in this field the degree name carries little information. In health care administration, neither the accreditor nor the federal statistical system distinguishes "management" from "administration", so the words are a marketing choice. That case is set out in two words, one code.

Health information management is the exception. Here a specific accreditor and a specific credentialing body have wired the programme name into the eligibility rules for two examinations. Getting the name wrong has a consequence you can point at.

The pathway, quoted

AHIMA lists the routes to the RHIA examination. The primary one is to successfully complete the baccalaureate-level academic requirements of a Health Information Management (HIM) program accredited by the Commission on Accreditation for Health Informatics and Information Management Education (CAHIIM).

Alternatives include completing the Master's-level academic requirements of an HIM program accredited by the CAHIIM, completing the academic requirements of an HIM Certificate of the Degree (Post-Baccalaureate) program approved by the CAHIIM, or graduating from an HIM program approved by a foreign association with which AHIMA has a reciprocity agreement.

For RHIT, the route is to successfully complete the academic requirements, at an associate degree level, of a Health Information Management (HIM) program accredited by the Commission on Accreditation for Health Informatics and Information Management Education (CAHIIM), or to graduate from a programme approved under a reciprocity agreement.

Every listed pathway runs through a CAHIIM-accredited HIM programme. That is what makes this a gate rather than a signal.

The exclusion nobody advertises

AHIMA is unusually direct about what does not qualify: Currently, AHIMA only accepts HIM degree programs to sit for the RHIA. Health informatics is not currently supported for the RHIA exam. The same restriction applies to RHIT.

This matters because "health informatics" and "health information management" are close enough in ordinary use that a prospective student may treat them as the same product. Both are legitimate fields. Both appear in university catalogues alongside each other. Only one is on AHIMA's list.

So the decisive question before enrolling is not "is this a good programme" but "is this programme an HIM programme, accredited by CAHIIM, at the level I need" — and the answer is checkable against CAHIIM's own record rather than the university's page. Where the two words in the technology half of this vocabulary come apart is set out in HIT, HIE, EHR sorted.

The third row is the whole difference. In administration, accreditation is evidence about a programme. Here it is a step in a procedure with a named examination at the end of it.

What CAHIIM does not promise

Worth stating carefully, because the previous section could be over-read. CAHIIM itself disclaims any guarantee: Once granted, CAHIIM accreditation will not guarantee or otherwise ensure student eligibility for credentialing examinations. All such matters are determined solely by credential-granting organizations.

Read together with AHIMA's own wording, the position is clear enough. Accreditation is necessary under every published pathway, and the credentialing body — not the accreditor — decides eligibility. Two different organisations hold the two halves, and a university can only speak to one of them.

The practical instruction: verify accreditation with CAHIIM, and verify eligibility with AHIMA, before relying on either. A programme page asserting both is asserting something it does not control.

Why this field has a gate and the others do not

The difference between this corner and the management side is not an accident of organisation. It follows from what the work is.

Health information work sits directly on top of legal duties: who may see a record, under what authority, within what deadline, and what must be logged. Those duties are defined in regulation and carry consequences when they are breached. Where competence is defined against a rulebook, a written examination can test it, and a credential can certify it.

Management competence is harder to examine. Running a service line well is judged on outcomes over years, against circumstances that differ by institution. There is no body of rules a written paper could test to establish that someone can do it. So the management side developed accreditation as a signal about programmes, and no examination gate at all.

The practical consequence for a student is a different kind of decision. On the management side you are weighing signals and prices. Here you are checking whether a specific door is open or shut, and the answer is binary. The underlying legal duties that make the work examinable are set out in who moves your record.

What the credential is evidence of

It is worth being clear about what a gated credential does and does not establish, because the strength of the gate can be over-read.

Passing a written examination after an accredited programme establishes that someone has been taught a defined body of material and can answer questions about it. In a field whose competence is largely rule-following, that is a meaningful signal about baseline knowledge.

It does not establish judgment under pressure, accuracy under volume, or the discretion to notice when a rule and a situation do not fit each other. Those are learned at work and observed by colleagues, not certified by anyone. An employer who treats the credential as the end of the assessment is making the same error in the opposite direction from one who ignores it.

What the destination pays

The federal occupation nearest to this credential is health information technologists and medical registrars: a May 2024 median annual wage of $67,310, with 41,900 jobs in 2024, 15 per cent projected growth to 2034 and about 3,200 annual openings. BLS lists an associate degree as typical entry and notes employers may prefer or require certification.

Two comparisons put that in place. Against the $49,500 all-occupations median it is $17,810 higher. Against medical and health services managers at $117,960 it is $50,650 lower — which is the gap the baccalaureate RHIA route and a move into management are aimed at closing.

The occupation is small, though, and that is the honest counterweight: 3,200 openings a year against 41,900 jobs is about 7.6 per cent turnover, healthy as a rate and modest in absolute terms. How a subject name can feed occupations with very different sizes and directions is worked through in which code.

Choosing the level, not just the programme

Because the credential follows the level, the sequence of the decision is unusual here: the target credential should choose the degree rather than the other way round.

Associate. A CAHIIM-accredited HIM programme at this level opens the RHIT route. It is the shortest and cheapest published path to a recognised credential in the field.

Baccalaureate. Opens RHIA. AHIMA also lists a post-baccalaureate HIM certificate approved by CAHIIM as a route, which matters for anyone who already holds a degree in another subject and does not want to repeat four years.

Master's. Also opens RHIA under AHIMA's listed pathways, and carries the graduate-level price. For someone whose objective is the credential rather than the degree level itself, the certificate route is worth pricing against it before committing.

Someone already holding an unrelated bachelor's degree therefore has three defensible paths to the same examination, at very different costs. That is a genuinely consequential fact and it appears on almost no programme page, because each page sells one of the three.

Four checks before enrolling

Is it an HIM programme by name? Not informatics, not health information systems. AHIMA's list is specific.

Is it CAHIIM-accredited at the level you need? Associate for RHIT, baccalaureate or master's for RHIA. Check CAHIIM's record, not the prospectus.

Which credential are you aiming at? The level of the degree determines which exam is open, so the target credential should decide the programme rather than the reverse.

Where does the price sit? Accreditation does not certify value for money, and tuition varies enormously between institutions of the same type — the figures are in half the door for bachelor level and five paths, one title for graduate level. Questions people actually ask

Does the accreditation of an HIM programme actually matter?

More than in adjacent fields, because it is tied to exam eligibility. AHIMA lists completing the baccalaureate-level academic requirements of a Health Information Management programme accredited by CAHIIM as a pathway to the RHIA exam, and completing an associate-level CAHIIM-accredited HIM programme as the route to RHIT. Without one of the listed pathways the exam is not open to you.

What do RHIA and RHIT stand for?

RHIA is Registered Health Information Administrator, which AHIMA describes as covering medical, administrative, ethical and legal requirements and standards related to healthcare delivery and privacy of protected patient information. RHIT is Registered Health Information Technician. RHIA runs from the baccalaureate level and RHIT from the associate level.

Will a health informatics degree work instead?

Not for these exams. AHIMA states that it currently only accepts HIM degree programmes to sit for the RHIA, and that health informatics is not currently supported for the RHIA exam. The same restriction applies to RHIT. Two degree names that sound interchangeable are not interchangeable here.

Which levels does CAHIIM accredit?

CAHIIM accredits health information management programmes at associate, baccalaureate and master's degree levels, and its accreditation is CHEA-recognised. That range is wider than the field's management-side accreditor, which operates only at professional master's level.

Does accreditation guarantee I can sit the exam?

No, and CAHIIM says so directly: once granted, CAHIIM accreditation will not guarantee or otherwise ensure student eligibility for credentialing examinations, and all such matters are determined solely by credential-granting organizations. Accreditation is a necessary step in the published pathways, not a promise from the accreditor.

What does the work pay?

The nearest federal occupation is health information technologists and medical registrars, with a May 2024 median annual wage of $67,310, 41,900 jobs in 2024, 15 per cent projected growth to 2034 and about 3,200 annual openings. The management occupation above it, medical and health services managers, had a median of $117,960.

How is this different from a health care administration degree?

In administration the degree name is not distinguished by the accreditor or by BLS, so it carries little information. Here the name is load-bearing: exam eligibility runs through CAHIIM-accredited HIM programmes specifically, and informatics degrees are excluded. It is the one corner of this field where checking the exact programme name and accreditation is decisive.
