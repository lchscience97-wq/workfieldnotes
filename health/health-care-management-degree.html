Health Care Management Degree: Two Words, One Code

Last verified
10 Aug 2026
Jurisdiction
United States
Data sources
CAHME; BLS May 2024 & OEWS; NCES 2021–22
Sources
5 cited
Reading time
9 min

"Management" and "administration" are not distinguished by anybody who governs this field. CAHME accredits programmes in healthcare management and lists MBA, MHA, MHSA, MPH, MS, and others as the degrees they award. BLS classifies workers by job duties, not their job titles, and both routes land in the same occupation code.

Which leaves three things that do vary and are checkable: accreditation — only 175 programmes hold CAHME status; price — a $31,164 interquartile spread among private nonprofit institutions; and the experience half of the entry requirement, which no degree supplies.

Who is answering, and what they cannot say

Every result on the first page for this term is a university programme page. Each describes its own curriculum accurately, and none can answer the question a prospective student is actually asking, which is whether this degree name is worth more than the one down the road.

A school cannot answer that, because a school only has one data point. The bodies that hold more than one — the accreditor and the federal statistical system — both decline to make the distinction at all. That is the finding, and it is worth more than any curriculum comparison.

What the accreditor says about the two words

CAHME describes itself as the ONLY organization recognized to grant accreditation to individual academic programs offering a professional master's degree in healthcare management education by the Council on Higher Education Accreditation, and states that it accredits 175 academic programs in healthcare management covering areas such as healthcare leadership, quality and safety, and population health.

Note what is absent. There is no separate accreditation for "administration" programmes and none for "management" programmes. There is one scope, described in one phrase, covering both.

The degree letters are equally unbound. CAHME notes that a variety of degrees reflect the disciplinary focus of the program such as MBA, MHA, MHSA, MPH, MS, and others. So accreditation attaches to the programme, not to the letters on the diploma — which means one MBA can hold it while another MBA does not, and the letters alone tell you nothing either way.

Only the last row moves with the naming at all, and it moves weakly: two programmes sharing a word can teach quite different things, and two with different words can teach the same things. The syllabus is the evidence; the title is not.

Where the naming actually comes from

If neither the accreditor nor the statistical system distinguishes the words, it is worth asking why programmes are named differently at all. The plausible explanation is institutional rather than academic: a programme housed in a business school tends toward management language, one housed in a health professions or public health school tends toward administration or health services language, and both then keep the name for continuity with their own alumni and marketing.

That is a claim about how organisations behave rather than a documented federal fact, and it is offered as such. What is documented is the absence on the other side: no accreditor scope, no occupation code, and no federal wage series treats the two words as different things. When a distinction exists nowhere in the records that govern a field, the burden sits with anyone asserting that it matters.

One occupation on the other side

Whatever the diploma says, the federal statistics record graduates arriving in the same place. Medical and health services managers had a May 2024 median annual wage of $117,960, with 616,200 jobs in 2024, 23 per cent projected growth to 2034 and about 62,100 annual openings.

The classification rule behind that is explicit: Under the SOC, workers are classified into occupations based on their job duties, not their job titles. A degree title is even further from a job title, so it cannot be doing classification work that job titles themselves do not do.

There is a corollary worth stating plainly. Because BLS reports by occupation and industry and never by degree held, no federal series can tell you what a management degree pays against an administration degree. Any page presenting that comparison is presenting a private survey. Which occupation code a given role actually sits in — and why that choice moves the median by $15,010 — is worked through in which code are you in.

The half a degree cannot supply

BLS records the entry profile as a bachelor's degree, less than 5 years of work experience in a related occupation, and no on-the-job training.

Employers typically expect prior work in a healthcare setting, in either an administrative or a clinical role in a hospital or other healthcare facility, with entry points including medical records specialist, administrative assistant and financial clerk.

There is a sequencing consequence worth stating. Someone already working in a healthcare setting is accumulating the second condition while they decide about the first, and can often study part-time against an employer contribution. Someone entering from outside the sector is buying only the first condition, at full price, and will still need the second afterwards. The same programme therefore represents two quite different propositions depending on where the applicant is standing, and no prospectus distinguishes between them.

The absence of on-the-job training is the sharpest of the three. An occupation with no structured entry programme is one that expects competence on arrival. So the master's is competing for a role whose posting already assumes healthcare experience — and the degree, whatever it is called, supplies one of two conditions.

Where a real difference can hide

None of the above says two programmes are interchangeable. It says the name does not tell you whether they are. Real differences exist and they are findable, but they sit in places a title cannot encode.

Quantitative depth. Some programmes run finance, accounting and operations analytics at a level that transfers to any sector; others treat them as service courses. That shows in the module list and the assessment format, not in the degree name.

Placement infrastructure. A residency, fellowship pipeline or structured internship addresses the experience condition described below. Its presence or absence is a larger practical difference between two programmes than any curricular emphasis.

Who teaches. A faculty drawn from practising administrators produces different coursework from one drawn entirely from career academics. Both are legitimate; they suit different applicants.

Each of these is checkable from published material before applying, and none of them correlates reliably with whether the word on the door is management or administration.

Price varies far more than the name does

NCES reports average graduate tuition and required fees for 2021–22, in current dollars, at $12,596 for public institutions and $29,931 for private nonprofit institutions.

The percentiles are where the decision lives. Inside the private nonprofit group the 25th percentile was $14,520 and the 75th was $45,684 — a spread of $31,164. Among public institutions the same spread is $5,522, from $9,292 to $14,814.

So the private nonprofit spread is 5.6 times the public one, and nothing in the accreditation record certifies that the expensive end teaches more. Set that against the degree-name question and the ordering is clear: the word on the diploma is free to choose, and the price attached to it is not.

Four checks that survive the naming

Is this specific programme CAHME-accredited? Check the programme against CAHME's own list, not the university's page. Institutional accreditation is a different thing.

Where does the price sit in the distribution? Against the quartiles, not the average. The 75th percentile is more than three times the 25th in the private nonprofit group.

Do you have the experience half? If not, the sequencing question — work first or study first — matters more than which programme you pick.

What does the syllabus contain? That is where any real difference between two programmes shows up. The wider comparison of five credential types across four accreditors is in five paths, one title, the job structure on the other side in where the administration jobs are, and the two-tier wage split of the sector in two tiers, $45,910 apart.

Questions people actually ask

Is health care management different from health care administration?

Not in any source that governs the field. CAHME accredits programmes in healthcare management without distinguishing the two words, and lists MBA, MHA, MHSA, MPH, MS and others as degrees its accredited programmes award. BLS classifies workers by job duties rather than job titles, so the naming choice does not change which occupation a graduate ends up in.

So does the degree name matter at all?

Less than three other things. Accreditation status is a matter of public record and is checkable. Price varies enormously between institutions of the same control. And the work experience requirement applies regardless of what the diploma says. The name is a marketing choice made by the school, not a signal recognised by the accreditor or the statistical system.

What does CAHME actually accredit?

CAHME states it accredits 175 academic programs in healthcare management covering areas such as healthcare leadership, quality and safety, and population health, and that it is the only organisation recognised by the Council on Higher Education Accreditation to accredit individual academic programs offering a professional master's degree in healthcare management education.

Which degree letters can carry that accreditation?

CAHME notes that a variety of degrees reflect the disciplinary focus of the program, such as MBA, MHA, MHSA, MPH, MS and others. So the accreditation attaches to the programme rather than to the letters, and an MBA can hold it while another MBA does not.

Does a management degree lead to a different job than an administration degree?

There is no federal evidence that it does. Both feed the same occupation, medical and health services managers, which had a May 2024 median annual wage of $117,960, 616,200 jobs in 2024 and 23 per cent projected growth to 2034. BLS reports wages by occupation and industry, never by degree title.

What is the requirement people miss?

Experience. BLS records the entry profile as a bachelor's degree, less than 5 years of work experience in a related occupation, and no on-the-job training. Employers typically expect prior work in a healthcare setting in either an administrative or a clinical role. A degree without that half satisfies one condition of two.

How much does a graduate programme cost?

NCES reports average graduate tuition and required fees of $12,596 at public institutions and $29,931 at private nonprofit institutions in 2021-22, in current dollars. Inside the private nonprofit group the 25th percentile was $14,520 and the 75th was $45,684, a spread of $31,164 for a nominally similar credential.
