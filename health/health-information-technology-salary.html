Health Information Technology Salary: Which Code

Last verified
11 Aug 2026
Jurisdiction
United States
Wage data
BLS, May 2024; projections 2024–34
Sources
5 cited
Reading time
9 min

"Health information technology" is a subject, not an occupation. Courses sold under that name feed at least three federal codes, and their May 2024 medians are $67,310, $73,340 and $60,340 — a $13,000 spread before anything else is considered.

Direction matters more than the spread. The health information code is projected to grow 15 per cent to 2034. The computer support group it is often confused with is projected to decline 3 per cent. Same course shelf, opposite trajectories.

Who is answering, and what they leave out

The results for this term are course marketplaces, training providers, a salary aggregator and one federal source. The training pages quote a number to justify a price; the aggregator quotes a number derived from its own postings. Neither states which occupation the figure belongs to.

That omission is the whole problem. A subject can feed several occupations, and the federal statistical system reports by occupation. So a number without a code attached is not wrong — it is unlocated, and an unlocated number cannot be checked or compared.

The classification rule that decides it

The federal wage programme states its own rule: Under the SOC, workers are classified into occupations based on their job duties, not their job titles.

A course name is one further step removed than a job title. If a job title does not determine the code, a curriculum title certainly does not. What determines it is what the graduate ends up doing all day, and that varies by employer.

Which is why the useful question before enrolling is not "what does this pay" but "which occupation do graduates of this course actually enter" — asked as an occupation name, not a subject.

Read the last two columns together. The best-paid row is projected to grow barely at all. The fastest-growing row is the smallest by a wide margin. And the two largest rows are both in projected decline.

Direction is the finding, not the spread

The pay spread across these codes is $13,000 from $60,340 to $73,340 — real, but ordinary. The projections are not ordinary.

BLS puts health information technologists and medical registrars at +15 per cent from 2024 to 2034. It puts computer support specialists at −3 per cent overall, split as +2 per cent for network support and −4 per cent for user support.

That is an 18 percentage point gap between the health information code and the combined computer support group, and a 19 point gap against user support specifically. Two occupations a prospective student might reasonably confuse are pointed in opposite directions over the same decade.

Growth rate against absolute size

Percentages flatter small occupations, and this pair is a clean illustration.

Health information technologists and medical registrars: 41,900 jobs, about 3,200 annual openings — roughly 7.6 per cent turnover. Computer support specialists: 882,300 jobs, about 50,500 annual openings — roughly 5.7 per cent.

So the declining occupation still advertises about 15.8 times as many openings a year in absolute terms, because it is roughly 21 times the size. A 15 per cent growth rate on 41,900 jobs is a smaller number of new posts than a 3 per cent decline removes from 882,300.

Neither figure is misleading on its own. Quoting one without the other is. The same percentage-against-base problem, applied to programme marketing, is set out in HIT, HIE, EHR sorted.

Why one grows while the other shrinks

The two projections are not arbitrary, and the reasoning is legible from the occupational descriptions themselves.

Health information work is generated by records, and the volume of records tracks the volume of care. Every encounter produces documentation that must be coded, checked, released under rules and retained. That workload does not fall when software improves; it changes shape. And the compliance layer around it — who may see what, under which authority, with which deadline — is defined by regulation rather than by convenience, which makes it durable.

General IT support is generated by problems, and the number of problems per user is what improves when systems mature, when software moves to managed cloud services, and when self-service tooling absorbs routine requests. An occupation whose volume falls as its subject matter improves is structurally different from one whose volume rises with activity.

That is a reading of the direction rather than a federal explanation of it, and it is offered as such. What is federal is the direction itself: plus 15 against minus 3, over the same decade, published by the same agency on the same basis.

What each route asks for

Entry requirements are closer than the wage figures suggest, which is part of why the routes get confused.

Health information technologists and medical registrars typically enter with an associate degree, and BLS notes employers may prefer or require certification, with credentials available from professional organisations.

For computer support the answer is split. BLS says network support specialists typically need an associate's degree, and user support specialists typically need to complete some college courses, adding that candidates may also qualify with a high school diploma plus relevant information technology (IT) certifications.

That last route is worth noting for what it implies about pricing. Where a high school diploma plus certifications is an accepted path, a two-year degree is competing against a cheaper alternative for the same entry-level roles. Where the credential is tied to an accredited programme, it is not — and in the health information family that tie is real, as set out in the one field where the name binds.

Where a certification changes the answer

One further variable separates these routes, and it runs in the opposite direction to the wage figures.

On the IT support side, certifications are vendor-issued and portable. They are bought, studied for and renewed independently of any degree, which is precisely why BLS can list a high school diploma plus relevant IT certifications as an accepted entry route. Portability is the feature; anyone can obtain them and they travel across employers and industries.

On the health information side, the recognised credentials are tied to accredited academic programmes rather than sold as standalone examinations. That makes them slower and more expensive to acquire, and correspondingly harder for a competitor to acquire quickly. Where a credential cannot simply be bought, holding it means more.

Neither arrangement is better in the abstract. They imply different strategies: on one side the cheap route is available to you and to everyone else, and on the other the expensive route is a barrier that works in both directions. The mechanics of that tie are set out in the name binds.

Where these sit against everything else

Against the $49,500 May 2024 median for all occupations, all four rows are above the national midpoint. Health information technologists at $67,310 sit $17,810 above it, about 36.0 per cent higher.

Against the healthcare sector specifically, the picture is different. Healthcare practitioners and technical occupations had a median of $83,090 and healthcare support occupations $37,180. The health information code sits between the two tiers, $15,780 below the practitioner tier and $30,130 above the support tier — which is a fair description of where the work sits organisationally too. That two-tier structure is examined in two tiers, $45,910 apart.

Reading a course page against this

Two phrases account for most of the ambiguity on training pages, and neither is untrue.

"Average salary." An average taken over a self-selected sample of postings is a different quantity from a federal median taken from an establishment survey. When the two disagree, they are usually measuring different populations rather than contradicting each other.

"In-demand field." Demand is an aggregate over occupations, and the occupations under this subject heading are moving in opposite directions. A page can describe the field as in demand while the specific occupation its graduates enter is in projected decline, without saying anything false.

Four questions before paying for a course

Which occupation do graduates enter? Ask for the occupation name, not the subject. If the answer is the subject repeated back, that is the answer.

What is that occupation's projection? The gap here runs from +15 to −4 per cent. It is not a detail.

How many jobs exist, not just how fast they grow? 41,900 against 882,300 is a difference in kind.

Is there a cheaper accepted route? Where certifications plus a high school diploma are accepted, the degree is competing on convenience rather than necessity. The parallel question for management credentials is worked through in two words, one code, and for the bachelor level in half the door. Which occupation code a management job falls into is in which code are you in.

Questions people actually ask

What does health information technology pay?

It depends which occupation the job lands in, because the phrase names a subject rather than an occupation. The nearest federal codes had May 2024 medians of $67,310 for health information technologists and medical registrars, $73,340 for computer network support specialists and $60,340 for computer user support specialists. The spread across those three is $13,000.

Which of those is growing?

Only one clearly. BLS projects health information technologists and medical registrars to grow 15 per cent from 2024 to 2034. Computer network support specialists are projected at 2 per cent and computer user support specialists at minus 4 per cent, with the combined computer support specialists group projected to decline 3 per cent overall.

Why do salary sites disagree about this job?

Because they are not all describing the same occupation. Under the SOC, workers are classified into occupations based on their job duties, not their job titles, so a course name cannot determine the code. When a page quotes a figure without naming the occupation it belongs to, it has chosen one of several answers without saying which.

How big is the health information occupation?

Small. BLS recorded 41,900 jobs for health information technologists and medical registrars in 2024 with about 3,200 annual openings, which is roughly 7.6 per cent turnover. Computer support specialists held 882,300 jobs with about 50,500 annual openings. The higher-growth occupation is the far smaller one.

What education does each route need?

Health information technologists and medical registrars typically enter with an associate degree. For computer support, BLS says network support specialists typically need an associate's degree and user support specialists typically need to complete some college courses, with a high school diploma plus relevant IT certifications as an alternative route.

Is the health information route better paid than IT support?

It sits between the two IT support codes. At $67,310 it is $6,970 above computer user support specialists and $6,030 below computer network support specialists. The clearer difference is direction: the health information code is projected to grow while the computer support group is projected to decline.

What should I ask before enrolling in a course?

Which occupation graduates actually enter, named as an occupation rather than a subject. Then check that occupation's own median, employment level, projected growth and annual openings. A course marketed on one subject can feed occupations whose outlooks point in opposite directions.
