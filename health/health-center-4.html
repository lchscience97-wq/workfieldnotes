Health Center 4 is at 4400 Haverford Ave., Philadelphia, PA 19104, with published numbers (215) 685-7601 and (215) 685-7605. It is run by the Philadelphia Department of Public Health.

The City publishes one long service list for its health centers and then tells you to call ahead. That is not vagueness. In the federal Health Center Program, services are approved for the organisation and sites are approved separately, on different forms — so a service can be genuinely within scope without being available in every building. This page explains that machinery and turns it into questions you can ask on the phone.

Health Center 4, in one place

The City of Philadelphia lists Health Center 4 at 4400 Haverford Ave., Philadelphia, PA 19104, in West Philadelphia, with two published telephone numbers: (215) 685-7601 and (215) 685-7605.

It appears among the centers the City describes as providing a full range of primary medical and support services to registered patients — the City's own wording is that eight City health centers provide a full range of primary medical and support services to registered patients, with one site on the roster limited to urgent care STD testing and treatment. The instruction before visiting is explicit: call ahead to make sure the service you need is available when you plan to visit.

The 19104 pair

Health Center 4 is not the only City centre in its ZIP code. Health Center 3, at 555 S. 43rd St., is also in 19104, with published numbers (215) 685-7504 and (215) 685-7506.

Two full-service sites roughly a mile apart is a practical asset rather than a redundancy: when one cannot offer a service on a given day, the other is the shortest alternative and the call-ahead instruction applies equally. Why the roster and the service list do not line up

The City publishes an unusually long list of services for its centers: chronic and acute illness care, checkups, screenings across several cancers, diabetes, cholesterol and hypertension, mammography, prenatal care, family planning and gynaecological care, pediatric care, dental care, behavioural health, medication for opioid use disorder, HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis, STI testing and treatment, immunisations, laboratory tests, x-rays, tuberculosis testing, nutrition counselling, tobacco cessation and social work support.

That list belongs to the programme, not to any one address. The underlying reason sits in the federal framework: HRSA defines a health center's scope of project as its approved sites, services, providers, service area, and medically underserved populations served. Sites and services are separate elements of that scope, approved separately, and the organisation is the holder of both.

Three forms, three questions

The documentation makes the separation concrete. A health center records its approved services on Form 5A, its approved service sites on Form 5B, and other activities and locations on Form 5C.

Form 5B carries the information for each site in the scope, including name, address and operational details. Form 5C covers activities that do not meet the service site definition, occur on an irregular timeframe or schedule and offer a limited activity from within the full complement of health center activities — health fairs, home visits, health education and immunisations are HRSA's own examples.

So the honest translation of a City service list is: this organisation is approved to provide these things somewhere in its scope. Which building, on which day, is a Form 5B and scheduling question, and the phone is where it is answered.

Directly, by contract, or by referral

There is a second layer that matters even more to a patient, and it is recorded on the same services form.

HRSA documents three delivery methods: services delivered directly by the health center, services provided through formal contracts and agreements, and services arranged through formal written referral arrangements. All three are legitimately within scope.

The consequence is that is dental offered? and is dental done here? are different questions with potentially different answers. A referral arrangement is a real service in scope terms and a second appointment somewhere else in practical terms. Asking which of the three applies is the single most useful clarification available on a first call.

What does not vary

The federal obligations attach to the organisation and therefore reach every approved site.

Section 330 of the Public Health Service Act, at 42 U.S.C. §254b(a)(1), defines a health center as serving a medically underserved population by providing required primary health services for all residents of the area served by the center. Subsection (k)(3)(G) requires a schedule of fees together with a corresponding schedule of discounts adjusted on the basis of the patient's ability to pay.

The City's own statements track those conditions: if you are uninsured, the centers charge a small fee based on family size and income, and the centers accept Medicare, Medicaid, HMO plans and most other insurance options. The City's Find a Clinic handout listing federally qualified health centers in Philadelphia includes the AHS Health Centers numbered 1 through 10 and the Strawberry Mansion Health Center, which is what places the municipal centres inside that federal framework. Four questions for the phone call

Is the service I need available at this site, and on which days? Is it delivered here directly, through a contract, or through a referral arrangement? Do I need to be a registered patient for it, or is it open to a single visit? And what will I be charged, given my insurance status and household size — may I see the sliding fee discount schedule?

Those four map onto the four things that actually vary: site, delivery method, patient status and price. A call that answers them has settled the visit; a call that only confirms opening hours has not.

If your number is not on the roster

The City's published sequence is not contiguous. It runs Health Center 1 through Health Center 6, then jumps to Health Center 10, and includes two centres listed by name rather than number.

A number in the gap is a legacy district identifier rather than a current listing, so searching for it returns third-party directories rather than a City page. Searching by street address, or calling the nearest listed centre and asking to be routed, is the faster route.

What this does not tell you

It does not tell you waiting times, which clinician you will see, or whether a specific service is running on the day you plan to arrive — that is precisely the residue the call-ahead instruction exists to cover.

It also does not tell you that a City centre is the right choice rather than one of the independent federally qualified organisations operating in Philadelphia, which carry the same federal obligations under their own governance. What the scope framework does explain is why a published service list and a specific building can honestly disagree, and what to ask so that the disagreement is resolved before you travel rather than after. Questions people actually ask

Where is Health Center 4?

The City of Philadelphia lists Health Center 4 at 4400 Haverford Ave., Philadelphia, PA 19104, with published telephone numbers (215) 685-7601 and (215) 685-7605. It is one of the City health centers operated by the Philadelphia Department of Public Health, and the City advises calling ahead to make sure the service you need is available when you plan to visit.

Why does the City say to call ahead?

Because in the Health Center Program a service is approved at the organisation level while sites are approved separately. HRSA describes a scope of project as the approved sites, services, providers, service area and medically underserved populations. Services are recorded on one form and sites on another, so an organisation can offer a service without every building offering it. The published list is the organisation's, not the address's.

What are Forms 5A, 5B and 5C?

They are how a health center documents its approved scope. Form 5A records the services provided, Form 5B records the approved service sites with the details of each, and Form 5C records other activities and locations that do not meet the service site definition, occur on an irregular schedule and offer a limited activity, such as health fairs, home visits, health education and immunizations.

Does offering a service mean it happens in that building?

Not necessarily. HRSA records three delivery methods on Form 5A: services delivered directly by the health center, services provided through formal contracts and agreements, and services arranged through formal written referral arrangements. A service can be genuinely within scope and still involve going somewhere else, which is why asking how a service is delivered is a different question from asking whether it is offered.

What is the nearest alternative to Health Center 4?

Health Center 3, at 555 S. 43rd St., is the other City health center in the 19104 ZIP code, with published numbers (215) 685-7504 and (215) 685-7506. Both are on the City's roster of centers providing a full range of primary medical and support services to registered patients, so if one cannot offer what you need on a given day the other is the shortest alternative.

What does not vary between the buildings?

The obligations that come with the federal designation. Section 330 of the Public Health Service Act requires service to all residents of the area served by the center and a schedule of fees together with a corresponding schedule of discounts adjusted on the basis of the patient's ability to pay. The City states that uninsured patients are charged a small fee based on family size and income, and that the centers accept Medicare, Medicaid, HMO plans and most other insurance options.

What if the center number I was given is not on the roster?

The City's published roster is not a contiguous sequence: it runs Health Center 1 through 6, then Health Center 10, plus two centers listed by name rather than number. Numbers in the gaps are legacy district identifiers rather than current listings, so searching by street address or calling the nearest listed center is more reliable than searching the number.
